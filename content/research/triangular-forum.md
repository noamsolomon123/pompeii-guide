# Triangular Forum + Doric Temple — Regio VIII

## Identity

**English name:** Triangular Forum (with the Doric Temple at its center).
**Italian name:** Foro Triangolare (Tempio Dorico).
**Regio/insula address:** Regio VIII, Insula 7, entrance no. 30 (VIII.7.30); the Doric Temple itself and the tholos/schola are grouped under the same insula (e.g. VIII.7.33 for the schola bench). ## What you see

- A monumental entrance (propylon) on the short northern vertex of the triangle: originally six Ionic columns in tufo with two engaged half-columns (lesene) and an architrave; a second doorway was cut in after the AD 62 earthquake, so what you see today is two adjacent doorways rather than the original single one. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
- A long colonnade of slender, closely-spaced Doric columns (95 in total originally) running around the three sides of the triangular precinct, leaving the south-west side open to a panoramic view over the plain and the Bay of Naples — this is the view a camera should catch from the temple platform. Source: [planetpompeii.com](https://www.planetpompeii.com/en/map/the-triangular-forum/460-the-triangular-forum-2.html); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
- The Doric Temple podium itself, near the south end of the precinct: a rectangular platform (~29 x 20 m) with a peristyle originally of 7 columns on the short sides and 11 on the long sides, in local Sarno limestone; today only the stepped/foundation courses and column stumps survive — it reads as a low stone footprint, not a standing temple. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
- A circular tholos shrine near the temple, built of seven Doric tufo columns around a "sacred well" (pozzo sacro), with an architrave inscription naming the magistrate Numerius Trebius as dedicator. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
- A semicircular stone bench (schola) with carved lion's-foot armrests, set beside the south-west corner of the temple, oriented to face the panoramic view; it originally carried a sundial on its back with a limestone inscription plaque. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VIII.7.33](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2007%2033.htm).
- A double-ring (concentric) low stone enclosure in front of the temple's main façade, of unresolved function — likely marking a grave or cenotaph, popularly linked to Pompeii's legendary founder. Source: [italyguides.it, Triangular Forum](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/triangular-forum) (cross-checked against the "Heroon" identification below).
- Three altars in tufo in one corner of the precinct, and a low boundary wall on the east side that once marked off a track used for horse/athletic contests. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
- What is NOT on site: the sundial inscription plaque from the schola is now in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN), inventory no. 3832. A decorated metopa depicting the myth of Ixion, originally from the temple's Hellenistic-phase entablature frieze, is also held at MANN, not displayed in situ. Sources: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VIII.7.33](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2007%2033.htm); [mediterraneoantico.it, "Nuova rilettura del mito di Issione"](https://mediterraneoantico.it/articoli/archeologia-classica/il-tempio-dorico-di-pompei-nuova-rilettura-del-mito-di-issione-trovato-su-una-metopa/).

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second version:**
This triangular terrace, perched on the highest, most panoramic point of ancient Pompeii, was already a sacred site before the city itself grew up around it — its Doric temple dates to the 6th century BC, centuries older than most of Pompeii's Roman-period buildings. By the 2nd century BC it had been reshaped into a grand colonnaded public square; the AD 62 earthquake damaged it further, and it lay half-buried and half-forgotten when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79.

**(b) 2-minute version (~150 words):**
The Triangular Forum takes its name simply from its shape: an irregular triangular plateau on Pompeii's southern edge, its widest side open to sweeping views over the Sarno plain and the Bay of Naples. At its heart stands the Doric Temple, built of local Sarno limestone in the first half of the 6th century BC — making it, by a wide margin, the oldest religious structure known at Pompeii, from the era when the settlement was a small Oscan/Samnite-influenced town within the wider orbit of Greek Campania. Ancient sources and inscriptions point to the temple being dedicated to Athena/Minerva, though a rival tradition also linked the spot to Hercules as the city's mythical founder. The temple fell into disuse well before AD 79, and by the time of the eruption the precinct functioned more as a scenic public gathering space — with porticoes, a shrine over a sacred well, and a stone viewing bench — than as an active cult center. It was damaged in the earthquake of AD 62, then buried under lapilli and ash in AD 79. Excavation of the area began in 1765. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2007%2030%20p1.htm).

**(c) ~250-word deep-dive:**
Archaeologically, the Doric Temple's building history runs across three main phases: original construction in the 6th century BC (specifically dated by some scholars to the third quarter of that century), a restoration in the 4th century BC, and a further, more limited restoration of the cella in the 2nd century BC following a long period of abandonment. Surviving column capitals compare stylistically to late-6th-century Magna Graecia temples, while some column shafts, in Nocera tufa, belong to the later Hellenistic-period rebuilding — meaning what visitors see is a genuine palimpsest of construction campaigns, not a single-period structure. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [madeinpompei.it](https://www.madeinpompei.it/2020/05/02/il-tempio-di-atena-al-foro-triangolare-uno-dei-primi-luoghi-di-culto-della-pompei-arcaica/).

The precinct itself was monumentalized in the second half of the 2nd century BC, when magistrates added the 95-column Doric colonnade enclosing the triangular plaza on three sides, along with the propylon entrance, the tholos over the sacred well (dedicated by Numerius Trebius), and — per pompeiisites.org's own 2016 University of Naples "Federico II" survey — a well-and-tank system in the north-west portico area that was apparently closed off during the Augustan age, plus votive pits dated to the 3rd century BC found near the so-called "Heroon." Source: [pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum](https://pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum/).

Scholarly debate on the deity is not fully settled: an Oscan-language inscription (catalogued as Ve 27) found near the temple supports a dedication to Minerva/Athena, and some scholars read the cella's double foundation as evidence of a joint Athena/Heracles cult, with Heracles venerated there as the (legendary, not historical) founder-hero of Pompeii — explaining the adjacent "Heroon" enclosure. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [madeinpompei.it](https://www.madeinpompei.it/2020/05/02/il-tempio-di-atena-al-foro-triangolare-uno-dei-primi-luoghi-di-culto-della-pompei-arcaica/).

Modern excavation began under Francesco La Vega between 1767–1782 (when the temple podium was first uncovered), continued sporadically in the 19th century, and became systematic under Amedeo Maiuri from 1931 onward. During WWII, Allied bombing of Pompeii in August–September 1943 (part of a campaign of over 160 bombs that hit the site, aimed at nearby road/rail lines rather than the ruins themselves) caused, per Italian Wikipedia, "notable damage to the northern section" of the Doric Temple specifically. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [pompeiiperspectives.org, WWII Bomb Damage](https://www.pompeiiperspectives.org/index.php/wwii-bomb-damage). Recent (2016-era, ongoing) fieldwork by the University of Naples "Federico II," under researcher Carmela Capaldi in agreement with the Archaeological Park of Pompeii, continues to sample the porticoes and sacred well area for pre-Roman stratigraphy. Source: [pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum](https://pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum/).

## The detail most visitors miss

Walk to the semicircular stone schola bench beside the temple's south-west corner and look at its carved armrests: they are shaped as lion's feet, and by at least 2005 one foot at the eastern end had gone missing (per photo-documentation on pompeiiinpictures.com) — a small, checkable detail of ongoing wear that most visitors, focused on the temple ruins and the view, walk straight past. The bench once carried a sundial on its back with an inscribed limestone plaque naming its donors, two duumviri (Lucius Sepunius Sandilianus and Marcus Herennius Epidianus) — that plaque is now in Naples (MANN inv. 3832), so what remains in place is only the seat itself. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VIII.7.33](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2007%2033.htm).

## Anecdotes

1. In front of the temple's main façade sits an odd double-ring stone enclosure whose function is not archaeologically certain; a long-standing tradition (not established fact) holds that it marked the tomb of Pompeii's legendary founder-hero. This is a **legend/tradition**, not a documented historical fact — sources describe the structure's purpose as genuinely unclear and only "probably" a grave. Source: [italyguides.it, Triangular Forum](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/triangular-forum).
2. The tholos shrine over the "sacred well" carries an inscription on its architrave naming the magistrate Numerius Trebius as the one who paid for and dedicated it — a rare case at this site of a named individual attached to a specific sacred structure, documented from the surviving epigraphic text itself. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).
3. The 2016 University of Naples "Federico II" excavation, conducted with the Archaeological Park of Pompeii, found that a well-and-tank system near the north-west portico had apparently been deliberately closed off during the Augustan age (late 1st century BC/early 1st century AD) — decades before the eruption — suggesting the sacred area was already being reorganized and partly decommissioned well before AD 79. Source: [pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum](https://pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/triangular-forum/).

## FAQ

**Q: Why is it called the "Triangular Forum" if it wasn't Pompeii's main forum?**
A: The name describes its shape — an irregular triangular plateau — not its civic function; it was a sacred/scenic precinct built around the old Doric Temple, distinct from Pompeii's main civic Forum in the city center. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare).

**Q: Which god was the Doric Temple dedicated to?**
A: Most epigraphic evidence (including an Oscan inscription, Ve 27) points to Athena/Minerva; a secondary and disputed tradition also links the site to Hercules as Pompeii's mythical founder-hero, and some scholars read the temple's cella foundations as evidence of a joint cult. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Dorico_(Pompei)); [madeinpompei.it](https://www.madeinpompei.it/2020/05/02/il-tempio-di-atena-al-foro-triangolare-uno-dei-primi-luoghi-di-culto-della-pompei-arcaica/).

**Q: Was the temple still in active use when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79?**
A: No — sources describe it as already abandoned/disused well before AD 79, and further damaged by the AD 62 earthquake; the forum area itself remained in public use as a scenic gathering space. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_Triangolare); [planetpompeii.com](https://www.planetpompeii.com/en/map/the-triangular-forum/460-the-triangular-forum-2.html).

**Q: Were any objects from here moved to the Naples museum (MANN)?**
A: Yes — at least the inscribed sundial plaque from the schola bench (MANN inventory 3832) and a carved metopa depicting the myth of Ixion from the temple's Hellenistic-period frieze are held at MANN rather than displayed on site. Sources: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VIII.7.33](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2007%2033.htm); [mediterraneoantico.it](https://mediterraneoantico.it/articoli/archeologia-classica/il-tempio-dorico-di-pompei-nuova-rilettura-del-mito-di-issione-trovato-su-una-metopa/).

## Hebrew naming

- **Triangular Forum** → הפורום המשולש (ha-Forum ha-Meshulash) — literal, commonly used descriptive translation; some Israeli guides also simply say "הפורום הטריאנגולרי" (ha-Forum ha-Tri'angulari), a direct transliteration, interchangeably.
- **Doric Temple** → המקדש הדורי (ha-Miqdash ha-Dori).
- **Tholos** (circular shrine) → תולוס (Tholos) — transliterated as-is, no standard Hebrew equivalent term in guiding use.
- **Cella** (temple's inner chamber) → קלה / צֶלָּה (Cella) — usually left transliterated; sometimes explained as "חדר הפולחן הפנימי" (the inner cult chamber).
- **Peristyle** (colonnade surrounding a court) → פריסטיל (Peristil).
- **Exedra / Schola** (semicircular bench/seating area) → אקסדרה (Exedra); the specific bench here is more precisely a schola (שקולה, transliterated) — Israeli guides commonly default to "אקסדרה" for any semicircular seating feature even when the precise Latin term is schola.
- **Propylon** (monumental gateway) → פרופילון / פרופילאה (Propylon/Propylaea) — transliterated, borrowed from Greek-site guiding terminology.
- **Ambulacrum** (covered colonnaded walkway) — not specifically documented for this site in the sources used, but standard transliteration is אמבולקרום (Ambulacrum) if referring to the covered portico walkway around the triangular plaza.
