## What you see

1. **The precinct colonnade** — a rectangular courtyard bordered on all four sides by fluted tuff (tufa) columns from Nocera. Originally these had a Doric entablature (triglyphs and metopes), but by AD 79 the columns themselves carried Ionic capitals that were, at the time of the eruption, being re-covered in stucco to look Corinthian — an unfinished mid-repair state frozen by the eruption. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Pompeii)) · [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p1.htm)
2. **The temple's own peristyle** — the raised cella at the centre of the court is itself surrounded by 48 Ionic columns (peripteros plan). [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Pompeii))
3. **The sundial column** — a single Ionic column near the entrance stairway that once carried a bronze sundial (meridiana), inscribed with the names of the two duumviri who paid for it (see History). [Archaeology & Art / CIL X 802 citation](https://x.com/archaeologyart/status/1276893662455218178) · [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm)
4. **The bronze statues of Apollo and Diana (copies in situ)** — on plinths flanking the temple steps, facing each other across the portico: Apollo in archer pose (bow now lost) and his sister Diana/Artemis, also shown shooting an arrow. **The bronzes you see on site are 20th-century replicas** (cast by the Fonderia Chiurazzi foundry, Naples, and installed in their find-spots in the early 20th century); **the ORIGINAL ancient bronzes are in the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN)** — Apollo, inv. 5629, and Diana/Artemis, inv. 4895. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm) · [Wikimedia Commons — inv. 4895](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Artemide_saettante,_dal_Tempio_di_Apollo_a_pompei,_inv._4895,_02.JPG) · [Wikimedia Commons — inv. 5629](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apollo_saettante,_epoca_romana,_da_tempio_di_apollo_a_pompei,_5629,_02.jpg)
5. **The white marble altar**, on a travertine base with sides in imported marble and a lava-stone tabletop, standing in the centre of the courtyard; its inscription names four quattuorviri (M. Porcius, L. Sextilius, Cn. Cornelius, A. Cornelius) who commissioned it by city-council decree — a formula pointing to a date shortly after Pompeii's re-foundation as a Roman colony (post-80 BC). [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm)
6. **The omphalos** — an ovoid stone sacred to Apollo, set inside the cella near its west wall, symbolising the sanctuary as a "navel" (world-centre) of the cult. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p3.htm)
7. **Painted decoration** on the courtyard walls — polychrome scenes (landscapes, architectural vistas, domestic and mythological subjects, including episodes from the Trojan War/Achilles cycle), and a griffins-festoons-foliage frieze on the converted entablature. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Pompeii)) · [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p1.htm)
8. **The cella floor**, with a mid-2nd-century-BC mosaic (green/white/black stone with a meander border) that carries the Oscan-language dedication naming the sanctuary's Samnite-era patron (see History). [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p3.htm)

## History in layers

**30-second version:** This courtyard, just off the Forum, is the oldest religious site in Pompeii — worship here goes back to the 6th century BC, centuries before the Roman colony existed. What you're standing in is a Samnite-then-Roman rebuild of an archaic Greek-influenced sanctuary, its bronze archer-god statues so famous that the originals now live in Naples' national museum.

**2-minute version:** Cult activity at this spot is attested from the 6th century BC, making it Pompeii's oldest identified sanctuary, with roots reaching back to open-air altars of the 8th–7th century BC. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei)) The Samnites rebuilt it as a proper temple, and a mid-2nd-century-BC Oscan inscription on the cella floor credits the quaestor Oppius/Ovius Campanus with paying for the work from Apollo's own offerings. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p3.htm) Under Roman rule the surrounding courtyard was enclosed by a colonnaded portico, and in the Augustan period a sundial was added and the precinct isolated from the neighbouring houses; the sanctuary hosted the Ludi Apollinares games. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei)) The earthquake of AD 62 badly damaged the temple, and repairs — including converting the old Doric/Ionic colonnade toward a stucco Corinthian finish — were still incomplete when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Pompeii)) · [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p3.htm) Excavation of the sanctuary began in **February 1817**, among the earliest systematic digs at Pompeii, with further campaigns in 1931–32, 1942–43, 1997 and 2015. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_(Pompeii)) · [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/sanctuary-of-apollo/)

**Deep-dive (~250 words):** The earliest phase is an 8th–7th-century-BC open-air cult area; by the 6th century BC this had become a proper terracotta-roofed building with decorated architectural terracottas, reflecting Greek and Etruscan religious influence in the wider Campanian region — the reason it is called Pompeii's oldest sanctuary. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei)) · [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/sanctuary-of-apollo/) The Samnite period brought a full rebuild, its date anchored by the Oscan floor inscription of quaestor Oppius (Ovius) Campanus (mid-2nd century BC), and by a later Latin building inscription naming duumviri M. Holconius Rufus and C. Egnatius Postumus who paid 3,000 sesterces to wall off the precinct. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei)) In the Augustan age an Ionic column at the courtyard stairway was fitted with a bronze sundial, its inscribed base recording that duumviri **Lucius Sepunius Sandilianus** and **Marcus Herennius Epidianus** paid for it at their own expense (CIL X 802) — the same pair also funded a sundial in the Triangular Forum. [Archaeology & Art citing CIL X 802](https://x.com/archaeologyart/status/1276893662455218178) · [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm) The famous bronze archer statues were unearthed in fragments starting **1817**, with the Apollo's missing pieces reportedly found only in **October 1818**, by soldiers hunting a fox near the city walls; on first discovery the marble/bronze fragments led excavators to misidentify the whole sanctuary as a Temple of Venus, then Mercury, before the Oscan inscription confirmed Apollo. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p2.htm) · [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/apollo_pompeii/) The Apollo bronze (MANN inv. 5629) and Diana/Artemis bronze (MANN inv. 4895) are considered products of the same workshop; the Apollo was sent to the Getty Villa, Malibu, for conservation in 2009 and exhibited there March–September 2011 ("Apollo from Pompeii: An Ancient Bronze") before returning permanently to Naples — it was never a permanent Getty holding, contrary to some inaccurate online claims. [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/apollo_pompeii/) No dedicated 2020–2026 restoration campaign specific to this sanctuary was found in available sources; recent scholarship draws mainly on the University of Michigan/Bryn Mawr "Pompeii Forum Project" urbanistic reappraisal of the sanctuary. [academia.edu — Pompeii Forum Project](https://www.academia.edu/68462271/Pompeii_Forum_Project_Excavation_and_Urbanistic_Reappraisals_of_the_Sanctuary_of_Apollo_Basilica_and_Via_della_Fortuna_Neighborhood)

## The detail most visitors miss

Set into the courtyard's white-and-green mosaic floor near the cella, an Oscan-language inscription — written right to left, in the pre-Roman Samnite alphabet — records that quaestor Oppius (Ovius) Campanus paid for the floor "with the money of Apollo" (i.e., temple offerings). It predates Pompeii's life as a Roman colony and is one of the clearest physical traces of the town's Samnite-era administration still visible in place; a related fragment with the same text is now in MANN (inv. 113398). Few visitors notice it underfoot while looking up at the statues. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p3.htm)

## Anecdotes

- **True, sourced:** When digging began here in February 1817, excavators did not immediately recognise the sanctuary as Apollo's — fragments of a marble Venus/Aphrodite statue and bronze arms found nearby led them to first label it a Temple of Venus, then briefly a Temple of Mercury, before the Oscan floor inscription settled the question. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p2.htm)
- **True, sourced:** The bronze Apollo's full anatomy wasn't recovered all at once — a year after the main find, in October 1818, soldiers out hunting a fox near Pompeii's city walls stumbled on more of the statue's missing parts. [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/apollo_pompeii/) *(Note: some retellings of this story vary in detail across sources; the Getty's own exhibition materials are the most authoritative version located.)*
- **True, sourced:** Modern conservation (Getty, 2009–2010) found the "original" Apollo bronze is itself a patchwork of ancient casting fixes and 19th-century restoration: a flawed ancient cast was mended with both "cold" hammered and "hot" poured bronze patches, and 19th-century restorers added iron-and-brass internal armatures plus drapery (later removed by Getty conservators and recreated in epoxy from archival drawings) to make the statue stand upright and look complete. [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/apollo_pompeii/)

## FAQ

**Are the Apollo and Diana statues here real ancient bronze?**
No — the figures you see flanking the temple are early-20th-century replicas (Fonderia Chiurazzi, Naples). The original ancient bronzes (Apollo, MANN inv. 5629; Diana/Artemis, MANN inv. 4895) are conserved in the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm)

**Why is there a sundial here?**
An Augustan-era Ionic column by the entrance stairway carried a bronze meridian/sundial, paid for out of their own pockets by duumviri Lucius Sepunius Sandilianus and Marcus Herennius Epidianus, per its surviving inscription (CIL X 802) — a typical act of civic self-promotion by elected magistrates. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2007%2032%20p5.htm)

**Why is this called Pompeii's oldest temple?**
Excavated evidence of cult activity here goes back to the 8th–7th century BC (open-air altars) and a built shrine by the 6th century BC, earlier than any other identified sanctuary in the town — making it the earliest known religious building at Pompeii. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei)) · [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/sanctuary-of-apollo/)

**Was this always a temple to Apollo, or was another god worshipped here first?**
Sources describe the site's origin as an early cult area with Greek/Etruscan-influenced worship rather than naming a different named deity before Apollo; the "Apollo" identity is attested by the Samnite-era Oscan inscription itself, so no separate earlier deity is documented at this exact spot in the sources consulted — flagged here as the limit of what could be confirmed. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/sanctuary-of-apollo/) · [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_di_Apollo_(Pompei))
