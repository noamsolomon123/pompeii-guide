## What you see

1. **A long paved rectangular plaza** — roughly 142–143 m long by 38 m wide, closed to traffic. Originally beaten earth, later paved in Nuceria tufa and finally, in the Imperial period, in travertine slabs. [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html), [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii)
2. **Stone bollards, raised kerbs and steps at every entrance** — large fixed stone blocks (e.g. three bollards and a high kerb on Via dell'Abbondanza) and a strategically placed fountain at the south-east entrance physically stopped cart traffic from entering; the raised paving itself acted as a further barrier. [erenow.org — Pompeii: The Life of a Roman Town](https://erenow.org/ancient/pompeii-the-life-of-a-roman-town/3.php)
3. **A two-tier colonnade (portico)** running along three sides — Doric-order tufa columns in the lower register, Ionic-order columns above; some lower columns were later replaced/upgraded in white limestone. [search synthesis citing architecture pages](https://www.planetpompeii.com/en/map/the-forum.html), [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html)
4. **Empty statue bases/pedestals** lining the square and its arches — not a single Forum statue survived in place. After the AD 62 earthquake the Forum became a permanent building site and most honorific statues were removed or never replaced. [seepompeii.com](https://seepompeii.com/en/luoghi/foro-di-pompei/), [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html)
5. **The Temple of Jupiter (Capitolium)** dominating the north end, deliberately aligned on axis with Mount Vesuvius, flanked by honorific arches. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/forum/), [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii)
6. **The Basilica** at the south-west corner (Pompeii's law-court/business hall, built c. 130–120 BC, one of the oldest known examples of the building type) and, at the south end, the municipal group — Comitium (voting hall) and a records building described in sources as a "tabularium." [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii), [general search synthesis](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii)
7. **The Macellum (food market)** just outside the north-east corner, and the Building of Eumachia (textile-guild headquarters) — both framing the plaza; the Macellum's imperial-cult statues were removed to the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN), and the building itself "had still not been fully repaired" by AD 79. [Wikipedia — Macellum of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
8. **The Granai del Foro** (Forum Granaries) on the west side — today an open-air storeroom/exhibit displaying over 9,000 artifacts (amphorae, pottery, tools) and several **plaster casts of eruption victims**. [seepompeii.com](https://seepompeii.com/en/pompeiis-forum-granaries-what-they-preserve-that-is-so-important/), [archaeoreporter.com](https://www.archaeoreporter.com/en/2021/07/25/the-forum-granaries-at-pompeii-the-secrets-of-ancient-trade-in-the-amphorae-of-via-dellabbondanza-video/)

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second intro.** The Forum was the beating heart of Pompeii — its civic, religious and commercial center rolled into one paved rectangular plaza. Temples, markets, courts and shops all opened onto it. When Vesuvius erupted in AD 79, the Forum was still a half-finished construction site, seventeen years after a major earthquake had wrecked it.

**(b) ~2-minute story.** The Forum began in the 4th–6th century BC as a simple open space ringed by shops, laid out under Samnite rule near the city's oldest sanctuaries. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_di_Pompei) After Rome absorbed Pompeii, the square was monumentalized in the 2nd century BC: surrounding shops and walls were cleared, a regular rectangular shape was imposed, and a two-story Doric/Ionic colonnade and tufa paving were added. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_di_Pompei), [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii) Under Augustus the pavement was upgraded again (travertine), the Macellum was rebuilt and an imperial temple added. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_di_Pompei) The earthquake of AD 62 badly damaged the Forum's temples and porticoes; repairs were still visibly underway — with damaged buildings, missing marble facing, and even an unrestored fallen statue — when Vesuvius buried the city in AD 79. [Wikipedia — Macellum of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html) The Forum was among the first parts of Pompeii to be systematically excavated, beginning in 1813 at the request of Caroline Bonaparte Murat, Queen of Naples. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/forum/)

**(c) ~250-word deep-dive.** The Forum was framed by Pompeii's most important public buildings: the Temple of Jupiter (Capitolium) on axis with Vesuvius at the north end; the Macellum food market and the Building of Eumachia (the wool-traders' guild hall) on the east side; the Basilica — the town's law court and business exchange, one of the earliest basilicas known, built c. 130–120 BC — at the south-west corner; and, at the south end, a cluster of municipal offices including the Comitium (voting hall), a town hall, and a building sources describe as housing the town's official records (a "tabularium"). [Madain Project](https://madainproject.com/roman_forum_of_pompeii), [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foro_di_Pompei) Dozens of statue bases for equestrian and standing honorific statues lined the colonnades and flanking arches — contemporary estimates describe room for seventy to eighty such statues — but not one survives in place today; most were removed, lost, or never replaced after the AD 62 earthquake. [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html), [ancientrome.ru](https://ancientrome.ru/art/artworken/img.htm?id=6776) No carbonized archive was found in the Forum itself; the city's best-known carbonized business archive, the 153 wax tablets of the banker Lucius Caecilius Iucundus, was found in his house in Regio V (V.1.26), not the Forum. [historyofinformation.com](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=4400) Scholars debate how much of the AD 62 damage was actually repaired by AD 79: one view holds Pompeii's civic center was in genuine decline and lacked resources to rebuild; archaeologist John Dobbins has argued instead that much of the apparent disorder reflects post-eruption looting and salvage rather than pre-eruption neglect; other researchers point to continued regional seismic activity through the 60s–70s AD as the reason work was still ongoing. [historyandarchaeologyonline.com](https://historyandarchaeologyonline.com/did-the-earthquake-of-62ad-lead-to-pompeiis-decline-or-renewal/) Current (2024–2026) restoration attention at the Forum centers on the Granai del Foro, where Pompeii's Archaeological Park has been re-arranging and re-displaying the thousands of artifacts and victim casts stored there since ancient times were excavated. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/notiziario-degli-scavi-di-pompei/lavori-per-il-riallestimento-dei-granai-del-foro/)

## The detail most visitors miss

At the **north end of the Forum, on the honorific arch immediately beside the Temple of Jupiter**, an equestrian statue once stood in a place of honor atop the arch. It fell during the AD 62 earthquake — and was simply never put back up. Seventeen years later, at the moment of the eruption, its empty perch was still empty. It is one of the clearest, most exactly locatable signs that Pompeii's most important square was still a construction site when Vesuvius erupted. [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html)

## Anecdotes

1. **TRUE.** Two carved marble bas-reliefs from the house of the banker Lucius Caecilius Iucundus (Regio V) are interpreted by scholars as an eyewitness depiction of the AD 62 earthquake in progress — showing damage to the Temple of Jupiter, the "Aquarium of Caesar," and the Vesuvius Gate. They are effectively an ancient Pompeian's snapshot of his own city's civic center being shaken apart, made 17 years before the final eruption. [Wikipedia — AD 62 Pompeii earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AD_62_Pompeii_earthquake)
2. **TRUE.** Systematic excavation of the Forum began only in 1813, ordered at the request of Caroline Bonaparte Murat, Napoleon's sister and then Queen of Naples — decades after digging had already started elsewhere at Pompeii in 1748. Excavators also found evidence the Forum area had already been "explored and stripped of its decorations" in antiquity — likely AD 79 survivors and looters recovering valuables before it was fully buried. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/forum/)
3. **TRUE.** The Forum's own grain warehouse, the Granai del Foro, has become Pompeii's largest single open-air storeroom: it now holds more than 9,000 excavated everyday objects — amphorae once carrying fish sauce from Spain and Africa, cooking pots, marble basins — alongside several plaster casts of eruption victims, turning the old Forum market building into an accidental museum of Roman daily life. [seepompeii.com](https://seepompeii.com/en/pompeiis-forum-granaries-what-they-preserve-that-is-so-important/)

## FAQ

**Q: Why couldn't carts drive through the Forum?**
A: The Romans deliberately engineered it as a pedestrian-only civic square: large fixed stone bollards, raised kerbs, steps, and even a strategically placed fountain blocked every entrance to wheeled traffic. [erenow.org](https://erenow.org/ancient/pompeii-the-life-of-a-roman-town/3.php)

**Q: Why are there so many empty pedestals around the square?**
A: The Forum once held dozens of honorific statues of emperors and prominent citizens, but not a single one survives in place today — most were destroyed, removed, or never restored after the AD 62 earthquake (for example, the equestrian statue atop the north arch, which fell in AD 62 and was never replaced); surviving related statuary, such as the Macellum's imperial-cult figures, is now displayed in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN). [romeartlover.it](https://www.romeartlover.it/Pompei3.html), [Wikipedia — Macellum of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**Q: Was the Forum finished when Vesuvius erupted?**
A: No. Archaeological evidence shows repairs from the AD 62 earthquake were still incomplete in AD 79 — the adjoining Macellum market, for instance, "had still not been fully repaired" — meaning Pompeii's main square was effectively a 17-year-old construction site when the eruption struck. [Wikipedia — Macellum of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**Q: What was actually bought and sold here?**
A: The Forum itself was ringed by shops and lined by porticoes for business and political life, while food trade was concentrated in the adjoining Macellum market — meat, fish (fish scales were found in its drainage channel) and other provisions for the city and surrounding villages. [gopompeii.com](https://gopompeii.com/pompeii-forum/)
