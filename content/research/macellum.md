## What you see

1. **Central open courtyard** bordered by a portico (originally tufa columns, largely rebuilt after AD 62) — the core of the market hall. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
2. **The tholos** — twelve tufa column bases arranged in a circle in the middle of the courtyard, once carrying a conical roof over a fountain/basin; this is the structure popularly (if imprecisely) called "the round temple." [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_(Pompei))
3. **Drainage channel/trough around the tholos** — the physical evidence (a stone gutter with fish bones and scales recovered from it) that anchors the fish-market interpretation. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
4. **Rows of tabernae (shops)** lining the west, north and south sides — roughly a dozen individual shop units opening onto the porticoes, some identified by finds (e.g. a shop in the south-east area linked to fish sale on the basis of the fish remains found there). [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-macellum-pompeii-en-72-m.htm)
5. **The elevated sacellum/shrine on the east side**, reached by five steps — a masonry room with wall niches that held imperial-family statuary; identified by pompeiisites.org as the imperial-cult chamber. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/), [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-macellum-pompeii-en-72-m.htm)
6. **Marble statue fragments/copies still associated with the shrine niches** — a female figure and an armed male figure, plus a probable emperor-statue fragment (Titus or Vespasian identification proposed), signalling the imperial cult use of the space. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)
7. **Fourth-style painted decoration remnants on the west wall** — fragments of mythological scenes (Odysseus recognized by Penelope; Io watched by Argus; Medea contemplating the death of her children) alongside still-life panels of birds, poultry, wine-jars, fruit, flowers, baskets and fish; these are in situ (what survives is the wall painting still on the Macellum's walls, weathered/fragmentary) — see History section on the MANN question. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
8. **A masonry counter with drainage holes** on the interior side identified as the meat/fish sales counter, next to the room used for the imperial cult's sacrificial banquets. [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-macellum-pompeii-en-72-m.htm), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**On MANN vs in situ:** Available sources describe the mythological Fourth-Style paintings (Penelope/Odysseus, Io/Argus, Medea) as decorating the Macellum's west wall itself — i.e., these are described as still visible/preserved on site, not as detached panels reassigned to MANN in the sources checked. Some capitals and architectural fragments recovered from the Macellum (e.g., a carved capital with an eagle motif) are explicitly noted as now held in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN). **This is a thinly-sourced point** — general MANN material confirms the museum holds large quantities of Pompeian wall paintings, mosaics and sculpture overall, but no source found during this research explicitly documents a Macellum fresco being physically detached (*strappo*) and moved to MANN, so guides should describe the mythological scenes as surviving (in fragmentary form) on the original walls unless a more specific MANN catalogue record is found. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2009%2007%20p1.htm), [museoarcheologiconapoli.it](https://www.museoarcheologiconapoli.it/en/portfolio-item/frescoes/)

## History in layers

### (a) 30-second intro
The Macellum was Pompeii's main covered food market, standing right at the busy north-east corner of the Forum. At its center stood a round structure surrounded by shops selling meat and fish, with a shrine to the imperial family tucked at the back. By the time Vesuvius erupted in AD 79, the building was still being repaired from a major earthquake 17 years earlier. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

### (b) 2-minute story (~150 words)
A market building already stood on this spot in the late 3rd century BC (Samnite period), but the structure visitors see today dates to a full rebuild around 130–120 BC, carried out as part of the wider reorganization of the Forum area. It was renovated again under the Julio-Claudian emperors, when it took on its final layout: a colonnaded courtyard with a circular tholos at the center, shops (tabernae) around the sides, and a raised shrine room for the imperial cult on the east side. The AD 62 earthquake badly damaged the building — the tufa colonnade had to be dismantled and shops rebuilt with fresh painted decoration — and repairs were still incomplete when the AD 79 eruption struck. The site was first uncovered by Bourbon-era excavators between 1818 and 1822, with further clearance in 1888. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_(Pompei)), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)

### (c) Deep-dive (~250 words)
**The tholos debate.** The round, column-ringed structure at the courtyard's center has been read in several ways. Early excavators, finding marble fragments and a circular colonnade, mistook the building for a temple or pantheon. Excavation directed by Amedeo Maiuri clarified that the tholos itself — supplied with running water and drained by a stone trough — was most likely a fish-cleaning and fish-selling station, based on the fish bones and scales recovered from its drain. Other scholars have floated alternative or additional functions for round market structures of this type across the Roman world — as a fountain, or as a stand for official weights and measures — and pompeiisites.org's own description hedges by calling it simply a structure "for the sale and cleaning of fish" without ruling out other uses. This remains a point of scholarly nuance rather than settled certainty. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)

**Imperial cult.** The elevated eastern chamber (sacellum), reached by five steps, held statues in wall niches — proposed identifications include Augustus depicted as Jupiter together with members of his family (Livia, Drusus, Tiberius, Germanicus), or later Neronian-period imperial portraits; a fragment possibly of Titus or Vespasian has also been proposed. Sources disagree on the exact figures represented. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_(Pompei)), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)

**Faunal evidence.** Beyond the fish bones/scales in the tholos drain, excavators recorded cereals and fruit on the north side, and sheep/ox bones near the association (banquet) room — plausibly linked to both market trade and sacrificial feasting. A specific peer-reviewed archaeozoological study of Pompeii fish remains exists for the nearby "Garum Shop" (Cavassa et al./De Simone research group; discussed at researchgate.net, publication ID 333383305), but this study concerns a different building (the Garum Shop) — no equivalent named, citable peer-reviewed faunal study specific to the Macellum's own fish deposit was located in this research; the fish-bone finding is reported consistently across encyclopedic and site sources but traced back to early-20th-century excavation reports (Maiuri) rather than a modern archaeozoological paper. **Flagged as moderately thin sourcing** for the specific faunal study citation. [researchgate.net (Garum Shop study, different building)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333383305_The_last_Garum_of_Pompeii_archaeozoological_analyses_on_fish_remains_from_the_Garum_Shop_and_related_ecological_inferences), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**Recent research/restoration (2020–2026).** No dedicated 2020–2026 restoration campaign or new research project specifically targeting the Macellum was found on pompeiisites.org during this research (checked Sept 2026); recent, well-documented Pompeii restoration announcements found instead concern other buildings (e.g., Casa della Fontana Piccola, Schola Armaturarum). **Flagged: no 2020–2026 Macellum-specific update found — treat this sub-point as unconfirmed rather than "nothing happened."** [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/)

## The detail most visitors miss

On the interior masonry counter near the fish/meat sales area (east side of the courtyard, beside the imperial-cult chamber), the drainage holes cut into the stone counter are original plumbing for running off water used to keep the fish fresh and wash down the counter — a small, easily overlooked physical trace of exactly how the stalls functioned day to day, distinct from (and more mundane than) the more famous painted decoration nearby. [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-macellum-pompeii-en-72-m.htm), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

## Anecdotes

1. **True, sourced:** Early 19th-century Bourbon excavators (digging 1818–1822) initially believed the Macellum was a pantheon or temple because of its marble fragments and the ring of columns at its center — the market interpretation only became clear once food remains (fish, cereals, fruit) turned up in the excavation. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_(Pompei)), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
2. **True, sourced:** It was excavation work associated with Amedeo Maiuri that settled the tholos's function as a fish-cleaning/selling station, based directly on the fish bones and scales found in its drainage trough — turning what earlier diggers had read as a shrine into a very practical piece of market plumbing. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)
3. **Legend/unverified — label explicitly as such:** Popular tourist retellings sometimes describe the round tholos as a "temple to the fish gods" or similar embellishment; this is not supported by the excavation sources reviewed here (which describe a practical market fixture, not a fish deity cult) and should be flagged to visitors as a folk simplification rather than documented fact.

## FAQ

**Q: Was this really a food market, not a temple?**
A: Yes — despite its temple-like round central structure and marble decoration (which misled early excavators), the discovery of fish scales/bones, cereals and fruit remains confirms its use as Pompeii's main covered food market, alongside a separate shrine room for the imperial cult. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**Q: What's the round structure in the middle?**
A: It's the tholos — twelve column bases that once held up a conical roof over a fountain/basin, most likely used for cleaning and selling fish (based on fish remains in its drain), though some scholars discuss other possible functions (e.g., weights/measures) for round macellum structures generally. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)

**Q: What foods were sold here?**
A: Primarily fish and meat, based on the counter with drainage holes and faunal remains found on site; excavators also recovered cereals, fruit, chestnuts, figs, legumes and other foodstuffs, some now in Naples museum collections. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii)

**Q: Why are there paintings of gods here, in a market?**
A: The mythological paintings (Penelope/Odysseus, Io/Argus, Medea) decorate the market's public walls as typical Fourth-Style decorative subject matter, while the separate elevated shrine room specifically housed the imperial cult — Roman public buildings routinely combined commerce with religious/dynastic display, and market oversight itself was a civic (state-linked) function. [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/macellum/)

## Hebrew naming

מאצ'לום (Macellum) — התעתיק העברי הרווח בוויקיפדיה העברית ובחומרי רקע הוא **מאצ'לום**, לא "מקלום". מקור: ערך "פומפיי" בוויקיפדיה העברית מתאר: "בפינה הצפון מזרחית של הפורום שכן המאצ'לום - שוק המזון".

מונחים נוספים:
- שוק מקורה — תיאור תפקודי נפוץ למבנה (Macellum כמוסד שוק מקורה רומי).
- תולוס — המונח הארכיטקטוני למבנה העגול המרכזי, משמש כפי שהוא גם בעברית (תעתיק ישיר מיוונית/לטינית tholos).
- מקדש הפולחן הקיסרי / סקלום (sacellum) — לפינה המקודשת שבצד מזרח.

הערה: לא נמצא במחקר זה שימוש מתועד ב"מקלום" כמונח נפוץ בקרב מדריכי טיולים ישראלים; ייתכן שזהו שיבוש/תעתיק חלופי בשימוש בעל-פה, אך המקור הכתוב (ויקיפדיה עברית) תומך ב"מאצ'לום". יש לדגל זאת כנקודה לא מאומתת אם המדריך מתכוון להשתמש במפורש במונח "מקלום".
