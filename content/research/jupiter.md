## What you see

1. **The high Italic podium** — roughly 17 m across the façade by 37 m deep (Wikipedia separately gives 121 × 56 × 10 feet, i.e. ~37 × 17 × 3 m), raising the whole temple well above Forum-floor level in the Italic (non-Greek) architectural manner. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-temple-of-jupiter-pompeii-en-71-m.htm)
2. **The grand staircase** — steps run the full width of the façade, with a wide central flight (site of the altar terrace) flanked by two narrower stairways leading up to the pronaos. [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-temple-of-jupiter-pompeii-en-71-m.htm) · [planetpompeii.com](https://www.planetpompeii.com/en/map/the-temple-of-jupiter/443-the-temple-of-jupiter.html)
3. **Six tall Corinthian columns** at the front, originally around 12 m high, opening onto the pronaos (entrance porch) before the cella. [audiala.com](https://audiala.com/en/italy/pompeii/temple-of-jupiter) · [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii))
4. **The triple cella** — the inner sanctuary was divided into three chambers, one for each god of the Capitoline Triad (Jupiter, Juno, Minerva); a triple cella is the defining architectural signature of a Roman "Capitolium" type-building generally, and is attested here specifically by the three acrolithic cult statues found in the temple. [Wikipedia — Capitolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolium) · [pompeii.uk](https://pompeii.uk/R7/7%2008%2001.htm)
5. **The two honorary arches flanking the temple** — the Arch of Augustus still stands at the temple's south-west corner; the Arch of Nero, at the south-east corner, was demolished in antiquity, and the spots where its piers stood are now marked by limestone paving slabs of a different pattern set into the Forum floor. [pompeiiinpictures.com — Arches](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Arches/Arches.htm) · [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/honorary-arches)
6. **The colossal marble torso of Jupiter**, reworked from an earlier relief block into a half-naked figure holding a sceptre and thunderbolt (Jupiter's attributes) — found here but **not on site today**: it is displayed in the "Campania Romana" gallery of the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN). Separately, marble cult heads of Jupiter (an older ~80 BC head, MANN inv. 6260, and a later replacement head modelled on Rome's Capitoline Jupiter statue by the sculptor Apollonius, MANN inv. 6266) and of Juno (MANN inv. 6264) also survive in Naples; the Minerva figure is lost. The identification of the reworked torso specifically as "Jupiter" rests on its sceptre-and-thunderbolt iconography rather than an inscription — treat the individual-head identifications as reasonably secure (they are cross-referenced by MANN inventory number) but the *torso's* precise original context/dating as more debated among specialists. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2008%2001.htm) · [pompeii.uk mirror](https://pompeii.uk/R7/7%2008%2001.htm)
7. **Underground storage chambers (favissae)** beneath the podium, used for sacred furnishings, votive offerings and possibly the city treasury — a reminder that this was a civic as well as a religious building. [pompeionline.net](https://www.pompeionline.net/en/archaeological-park-of-pompeii/temples-of-pompeii) · [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-temple-of-jupiter-pompeii-en-71-m.htm)
8. **Visible earthquake/incomplete-repair evidence** — multiple sources agree the temple was still not fully restored to its pre-62 AD appearance when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79, i.e. the structure visitors see today is itself an unfinished building site frozen in time (see History). [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [pompeionline.net](https://www.pompeionline.net/en/archaeological-park-of-pompeii/temples-of-pompeii)

## History in layers

**30-second version:** This temple, towering over the north end of the Forum with Vesuvius framed directly behind it, began as a shrine to Jupiter alone in the mid-2nd century BC. After Pompeii became a Roman colony in 80 BC it was rededicated as a Capitolium — a temple to Jupiter, Juno and Minerva together — modelled on the great Temple of Jupiter on Rome's Capitoline Hill, a deliberate statement of loyalty to Rome. It was still a damaged building site, only partly repaired after a major earthquake, when the eruption buried it in AD 79.

**2-minute version:** The temple was built around the mid-2nd century BC (some sources say specifically c. 150 BC) and originally honoured Jupiter alone, in the "pure Italic" style typical of pre-Roman central Italy. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [pompeii-tickets.com](https://www.pompeii-tickets.com/the-forum/) Following Pompeii's forced Roman colonization under Sulla in 80 BC, the temple was enlarged and its cella divided in three, becoming a Capitolium dedicated to the Capitoline Triad — Jupiter, Juno and Minerva — the standard state-religion package Rome imposed on its colonies. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [pompeii.uk](https://pompeii.uk/R7/7%2008%2001.htm) Raised on its high Italic podium with a grand frontal staircase and six Corinthian columns, it dominated the Forum visually and symbolically. [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/m.php/museum-temple-of-jupiter-pompeii-en-71-m.htm) The earthquake of AD 62 badly damaged the building; in its aftermath the smaller, older Temple of Jupiter Meilichios temporarily took over as the city's main cult centre, and archaeological and textual evidence agrees that full restoration of the Capitolium had still not been completed seventeen years later, when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [audiala.com](https://audiala.com/en/italy/pompeii/temple-of-jupiter) The structure was excavated in three main campaigns — 1810, 1816 and 1820 — among the earliest parts of Pompeii to be cleared. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2008%2001.htm)

**Deep-dive (~250 words):** Before 80 BC, Pompeii was an independent Samnite town, and the temple on this spot most likely already served a civic-religious function; scholarship treats the mid-2nd-century-BC building phase as Samnite-period construction later absorbed into the Roman scheme rather than a wholly new Roman foundation. [Wikidata Q3309400](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q3309400) · [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) After the Social War, Sulla settled his veterans here and refounded the city as Colonia Cornelia Veneria Pompeianorum; converting the existing temple into a Capitolium — with its signature triple cella for Jupiter, Juno and Minerva — was among the clearest architectural markers of that new Roman political identity, since a Capitolium modelled on Rome's own Capitoline Hill sanctuary was the standard religious "seal" Rome placed on its colonies across Italy and the provinces. [Wikipedia — Capitolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolium) The temple functioned as both shrine and civic strongroom: beneath the podium, storage chambers (favissae) held votive offerings, sacred furnishings, and reportedly the city treasury. [pompeionline.net](https://www.pompeionline.net/en/archaeological-park-of-pompeii/temples-of-pompeii) Three acrolithic statues — marble heads and hands on wood-and-drapery bodies — represented the triad; an older cult head of Jupiter (c. 80 BC, MANN inv. 6260) was later supplemented or replaced by a head modelled on the famous Capitoline Jupiter of the sculptor Apollonius, made after Rome's own Capitoline temple burned in 83 BC (MANN inv. 6266), alongside a surviving head of Juno (MANN inv. 6264); the Minerva figure has not survived. A separately reworked colossal marble torso of Jupiter, carved from a repurposed relief block, is now displayed in MANN's "Campania Romana" gallery — its exact original placement and full attribution remain matters of specialist discussion rather than settled fact. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2008%2001.htm) No dedicated 2020–2026 restoration campaign specific to the Capitolium building itself was located in the sources consulted for this file.

## The detail most visitors miss

At the temple's south-east corner (as you face the temple from the Forum, on the right-hand side), look at the Forum's paving: the limestone slabs there are laid in a different pattern from the surrounding pavement. This is not decorative — it marks the footprint of the vanished Arch of Nero, torn down in antiquity, most likely in the aftermath of Nero's death and official condemnation of memory (damnatio memoriae) in AD 68, or simply to open the sightline to the arch behind it. It is one of the few places in Pompeii where you can "read" a deliberate act of political erasure directly in the ground plan. ## Anecdotes

- **True, sourced:** A marble relief found in the lararium (household shrine) of the House of Caecilius Iucundus depicts the Temple of Jupiter and the Vesuvius Gate area appearing to collapse during an earthquake — it is treated by scholars as a rare "eyewitness" visual record of the AD 62 earthquake's effect on this very building, made by residents who lived through it. [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/honorary-arches) · [planetpompeii.com](https://www.planetpompeii.com/en/map/the-temple-of-jupiter/443-the-temple-of-jupiter.html)
- **True, sourced:** According to archaeologist John Dobbins (cited via pompeiiinpictures.com), even now "the precise dates [of the two honorary arches], the date of removal of [the Arch of Nero], and the individuals to whom they were dedicated are unknown" — a reminder that even a monument standing in plain sight at the Forum's most photographed spot still holds real, acknowledged scholarly uncertainty. [pompeiiinpictures.com — Arches](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Arches/Arches.htm)
- **True, sourced:** The replacement cult head of Jupiter placed in the Pompeii Capitolium (MANN inv. 6266) was deliberately modelled on the famous Capitoline Jupiter statue by the Greek sculptor Apollonius in Rome — itself a replacement made after Rome's own original Capitoline temple burned down in 83 BC — meaning Pompeii's Jupiter image consciously echoed, one Roman generation later, the very same disaster-and-rebuild story its own temple would go on to repeat after the AD 62 earthquake. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2008%2001.htm)

## FAQ

**Why is it called the "Capitolium" and not just the "Temple of Jupiter"?**
"Capitolium" is the standard Roman term for a temple to the Capitoline Triad (Jupiter, Juno, Minerva), modelled on the original Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus on Rome's Capitoline Hill; Pompeii's building earned the name once it was rededicated to all three gods after the city became a Roman colony in 80 BC. [Wikipedia — Capitolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolium) · [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii))

**Which gods were actually worshipped here?**
Originally Jupiter alone; after 80 BC, the Capitoline Triad — Jupiter, Juno and Minerva — each with a dedicated chamber in the temple's triple cella. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [Wikipedia — Capitolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolium)

**Why does it already look so damaged, before you even think about the eruption?**
The temple suffered severe structural damage in the earthquake of AD 62, and multiple sources agree repairs were still not complete when Vesuvius erupted 17 years later in AD 79 — visitors are looking at an unfinished 1st-century construction site, not a "frozen intact" building. [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_(Pompeii)) · [audiala.com](https://audiala.com/en/italy/pompeii/temple-of-jupiter)

**Where is the giant statue of Jupiter now?**
The colossal marble torso of Jupiter found at the temple is on display in the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN), in the "Campania Romana" gallery — it is not at the Pompeii site itself; related cult heads (Jupiter, Juno) are also in MANN's collection. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2008%2001.htm)
