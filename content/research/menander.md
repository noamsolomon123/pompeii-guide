## What you see

- The **Menander portrait exedra**: a small room/alcove off the north side of the peristyle holding the seated portrait of the playwright Menander in a central niche — the house's namesake image. **On-site**, painted directly on the wall (not a MANN-held original) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander).
- The **peristyle garden**: a large Doric-columned garden court, one of the most complete in Pompeii, with a "rhomboid"-shaped plan and paintings referencing the Trojan War cycle in the atrium — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/casa-del-menandro/).
- The **private bath suite**: a small independent thermal complex with its own 8-columned mini-atrium, including a heated room with a mosaic floor of acanthus leaves and marine creatures — [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/casa-del-menandro/).
- The **corridor/cellar where the silver treasure was found**, adjoining the small atrium of the baths — the physical findspot is on-site, but the silver itself is **not** here (see below) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander).
- A **lararium** (household shrine) in the peristyle area — [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/casa-del-menandro/) confirms a lararium is present in the peristylium; note the "owner depicted as a soldier" detail on the shrine could not be independently confirmed in the sources reviewed and should be treated as unverified pending further checking.
- A small **library/study room** (~10 sq m) on the south side of the peristyle with wall niches for scroll storage — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander).
- Fine **Second- and Fourth-Style frescoes** throughout, including a depiction connected to the Laocoön myth — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander), [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro).
- **The silver "Menander Treasure" itself (118 pieces) is NOT on display at the house** — it is held at the **Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN)**, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander) and [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro). What visitors see in the house is the empty findspot, not the objects.

## History in layers

### 30-second version
This is one of the largest and most luxurious private houses ever excavated at Pompeii, covering roughly 1,800 square metres with its own private bath suite. It gets its modern name from a painted portrait of the Greek playwright Menander found in a small room off its garden. Beneath the baths, excavators found a hoard of buried silver tableware — one of the richest such hoards ever recovered from the site.

### 2-minute version
The House of Menander began as a modest atrium house around 250 BC and was expanded over centuries into an elite residence of roughly 1,800 sq m, eventually gaining a peristyle garden and a private bath complex in the Augustan period ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). Its last known owner is tied to the name "Quintus Poppaeus," known from a seal found on the property — some scholars have linked this family, the Poppaei, to Poppaea Sabina, Nero's second wife, though this remains a debated hypothesis rather than settled fact ([Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro); [Wikipedia — Poppaea Sabina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppaea_Sabina)). In a corridor beneath the small atrium of the private baths, excavators found 118 silver vessels wrapped in cloth and stored in a wooden chest, along with gold items and 1,432 sesterces in coin — one of the best-preserved silver hoards from the Vesuvian sites ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). Eighteen victims of the AD 79 eruption were found in the house, including a group in a corridor carrying lamps, pickaxes and shovels — thought to be looters caught inside rather than residents — plus a dog ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). The house was excavated by Amedeo Maiuri between November 1926 and June 1932, and published in his 1932 monograph *La casa del Menandro e il suo tesoro di argenteria* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)).

### Deep dive (~250 words)
The House of Menander is treated by scholars as a benchmark for understanding elite domestic life at Pompeii: its scale (~1,800 sq m), its private thermal suite, its extensive service and storage wings, and its decorative program all point to a family of high status. The portrait that gives the house its name shows a seated figure identified as Menander, the 4th–3rd century BC Athenian comic playwright, painted in a small exedra off the peristyle; some scholars have floated the alternative reading that the figure is the owner himself shown reading Menander's works rather than the poet in person, though the conventional identification as Menander is what has stuck as the house's name ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). The silver hoard — 118 pieces including cups, spoons, and other tableware, plus gold objects and 1,432 sesterces — was found hidden in a wooden chest in an underground space reached through the bath complex, evidently concealed in haste; it is now held at the MANN in Naples ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander); [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro)). Ownership is tied, via a seal bearing the name Quintus Poppaeus (or "Quintus Poppaeus Sabinus" per Matteo Della Corte's early attribution), to the Poppaei family; the popular claim that this connects the house to Empress Poppaea Sabina is explicitly flagged by Wikipedia as speculative ("very likely," "common belief," "might have been") and is NOT an established fact ([Wikipedia — Poppaea Sabina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppaea_Sabina)). Eighteen eruption victims were recovered across several rooms and a corridor, some apparently looters caught in the act, evidenced by the tools they carried ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). Later scholarship, notably the British School at Rome / University of Bradford's Anglo-American Project in Pompeii (initiated 1978) under Roger Ling, re-surveyed the whole insula and published a multi-volume re-study (Ling 1997 on the structures; Allison 2006 on the finds), superseding Maiuri's original 1930s account as the authoritative modern record ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)). No specific 2020–2026 restoration or new-research announcement for this house could be confirmed in the sources checked for this note.

## The detail most visitors miss

In the small library/study room on the south side of the peristyle (only about 10 sq m), look for the wall niches once used for scroll storage — an easily overlooked reminder that this was a house of literary, book-owning pretensions, matching the learned Greek theme of the Menander portrait itself ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander)).

## Anecdotes

- The 118-piece silver hoard was found stuffed into a wooden chest in an underground space off the bath corridor — clearly hidden in a hurry, alongside gold objects and 1,432 sesterces in coin, suggesting the household tried to save its valuables during the eruption's early phase. **True, sourced fact.** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander))
- Ten of the eighteen eruption victims found in the house were discovered together in a corridor carrying lamps, pickaxes and shovels — interpreted as looters who broke in after the eruption began (or during a lull) and were killed before they could escape. **True, sourced fact**, interpretation ("looters") as reported by the source. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander))
- The claim that this house belonged to relatives of Empress Poppaea Sabina, Nero's second wife, rests on a single seal bearing the name "Quintus Poppaeus" and is explicitly described in sources as a popular but unproven theory — **flagged here as a disputed hypothesis, not a settled fact.** ([Wikipedia — Poppaea Sabina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppaea_Sabina); [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Menandro))

## FAQ

1. **Is the silver treasure on display here?** No. The 118-piece silver hoard found in the house's cellar is held at the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN), not at the house itself. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander))
2. **Was this really the house of Nero's wife's family?** It's debated. A seal naming "Quintus Poppaeus" links the house to the Poppaei family, and some scholars connect that family to Empress Poppaea Sabina — but this is a hypothesis, not confirmed fact. ([Wikipedia — Poppaea Sabina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppaea_Sabina))
3. **Why is it called the "House of Menander"?** Because a wall painting of the Athenian comic playwright Menander was found in a small room off the peristyle garden — the house is named for that portrait, not for its actual owner. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander))
4. **Who excavated it and when?** Amedeo Maiuri excavated the house between November 1926 and June 1932, publishing his findings in 1932; the British School at Rome / University of Bradford's Anglo-American Project later re-studied the whole insula starting in 1978. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Menander))
