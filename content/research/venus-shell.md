## What you see

- **The Venus fresco itself** — a roughly 10-meter-wide painting across the peristyle garden's south (back) wall: Venus reclining inside a giant scallop shell, nude except for a diadem on her head and jewelry at neck, wrists and ankles. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)
- **Two cupids (amorini)** flanking her — one riding a dolphin, the other shown supporting/steadying the shell itself. [pompeiiinpictures.com, part 11](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20part%2011.htm)
- **Mars on the left**, standing on a pedestal as Pompeii's other protector deity, with a heron painted beside him. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [pompeiiinpictures.com, part 11](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20part%2011.htm)
- **A marble fountain on the right**, with birds shown drinking from it, plus a theatrical mask and a garden panel with ferns and further birds in the adjoining west panel. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [pompeiiinpictures.com, part 11](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20part%2011.htm)
- **A painted garden scene below/around the main panel**, with exotic plants and animals — a common Pompeian device for extending a real garden's greenery into a painted one. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/)
- **The peristyle-garden layout itself:** rooms arranged around the garden rather than around a traditional tablinum-centered plan — the same design choice as the House of the Vettii — with a north, west and south portico. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [pompeiiinpictures.com plan](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20plan.htm)
- **An unusually large oecus (reception room)** — described as second in size in Pompeii only to the House of Menander's — but note it was left unfinished (see "detail most visitors miss" below). [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [pompeiiinpictures.com quickie page](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20quickie.htm)
- **Atrium with impluvium** and a decorated entrance corridor (fauces) with painted medallions on its east and west walls. [pompeiiinpictures.com, part 1](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20part%201.htm)
- **Original vs. restored vs. moved:** the Venus fresco remains **in situ** on the garden wall (it was not removed to Naples), but it underwent a major conservation campaign completed in **2016**; separately, parts of the house's structure were damaged by Allied bombing in **1943**, part of wider WWII damage across the excavated city. No objects from this specific house being moved to MANN Naples were identified in the sources consulted for this file. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)

## History in layers

### (a) 30-second intro
This 1st-century-BC Pompeian house is known for one image: a roughly 10-meter fresco of Venus reclining in a giant scallop shell, painted across the back wall of its garden. Venus was not a random choice of subject — she was Pompeii's own patron deity, making this one of the most photographed surviving images from the ancient city. Remarkably, part of the house itself — its grand reception room — was still mid-renovation, bare walls and all, when Vesuvius erupted.

### (b) ~150 words, 2-minute story
The house stood in Regio II, near Via dell'Abbondanza, and belonged to a branch of the wealthy Satrii family, prominent in Pompeii's final decades. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/) It was remodeled in the 1st century BC to center on a peristyle garden rather than a traditional tablinum-focused plan, like the House of the Vettii, and gained an oversized reception room (oecus) — second in Pompeii only to the House of Menander's — which, at the time of the eruption, had only its black-and-white mosaic floor laid and bare, undecorated walls "because it had only just been added to the house." [pompeiiinpictures.com quickie page](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20quickie.htm) Vesuvius's AD 79 eruption — now dated by a 2022 interdisciplinary study to **24–25 October** rather than the traditionally assumed August — buried the house along with the rest of the city. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) Excavation came relatively late for Pompeii, in two campaigns: **1933–1935 and 1951–1953**. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)

### (c) ~250 words deep-dive
The Venus fresco is more than a decorative flourish. After Rome's dictator Sulla settled army veterans in Pompeii following the Social War (89 BC), the town was refounded as a Roman colony under the name **"Colonia Cornelia Veneria Pompeianorum"** — a name built around Venus as the city's patron deity. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) Painting a monumental Venus — nude but for a diadem and jewelry, cradled in a shell with attendant cupids (one on a dolphin, one steadying the shell) and Mars standing guard on a pedestal alongside — turned a private garden wall into a statement of civic-religious piety as much as of the owners' taste and wealth. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/); [pompeiiinpictures.com, part 11](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20part%2011.htm) The fresco is generally placed among the large mythological garden set-pieces typical of Pompeii's final decades before AD 79, though the sources consulted for this file did not offer a fully worked-out formal "Style" (First–Fourth) classification specific to this painting, since garden/peristyle wall paintings are sometimes discussed separately from interior-room fresco sequences — treat any single style label applied to it as approximate rather than settled. Being an exterior, open-air garden painting has made it unusually exposed to weathering compared with interior frescoes, and it underwent a major restoration completed in **2016**. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia) The house's fabric — not specifically this wall — sustained damage in Allied bombing raids on Pompeii in **1943**. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia) No inscriptions or graffiti from this specific house were identified in the English/Italian Wikipedia articles or the pompeiiinpictures.com photo documentation reviewed for this file — if any exist, they were not covered by these sources; flag as unresolved and worth on-site/guide verification. No 2020–2026 conservation or study work specific to this house (beyond the 2016 fresco restoration) was found in this research.

## The detail most visitors miss

The house's grand reception room (the **oecus**, opening off the west side of the atrium) looks, at first glance, like just an empty room next to the spectacular Venus wall — but it is frozen mid-renovation. Per the on-site information board (as transcribed by pompeiiinpictures.com), only the black-and-white mosaic floor had been laid, and the walls were left undecorated "because it had only just been added to the house" — meaning this was an active, unfinished construction/redecoration project, very possibly post-AD-62-earthquake repair work, interrupted by the AD 79 eruption. Look for the bare plaster and the incomplete floor pattern as you pass from the atrium toward the peristyle. [pompeiiinpictures.com quickie page](http://www.pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R2/2%2003%2003%20quickie.htm)

## Anecdotes

1. **A masterpiece that outlasted a world war, then got fixed.** The house sustained structural damage in the 1943 Allied bombing of Pompeii; the Venus fresco itself nonetheless survived and, decades later, underwent a full modern conservation campaign completed in 2016. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)
2. **Dug up in two separate stints, decades apart.** Unlike houses excavated in one continuous 19th-century campaign, this house was brought to light in two distinct periods — 1933–1935, then again 1951–1953 — nearly two centuries after Pompeii's excavations first began in the 18th century. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/)
3. **The "default" image of Venus.** The Italian Wikipedia's general article on the goddess Venus illustrates itself with a photo of this exact Pompeii fresco, captioned simply "Venus, reclining on a seashell, painted in a fresco from Pompeii" — this garden wall functions, in effect, as a default visual shorthand for the goddess in Italian reference material. [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venere_(divinità)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venere_(divinit%C3%A0))

No legends were identified for this house in the sources consulted; all three items above are sourced facts, not folklore.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the fresco I'm looking at the original Roman painting, or a copy?**
A: It is the original ancient fresco, still in its original location on the peristyle's south wall — it was never removed to a museum — though it underwent a major restoration completed in 2016 to stabilize and conserve the original paint layer. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)

**Q: Why is Venus shown in a giant shell here specifically?**
A: The shell evokes Venus/Aphrodite's mythic birth from the sea, and Pompeii itself was officially a Roman colony named for Venus — "Colonia Cornelia Veneria Pompeianorum" — making her the city's patron deity; a Venus image in a private garden was as much civic piety as personal taste. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii)

**Q: Was this house excavated recently, compared to other Pompeii sites?**
A: Relatively yes — it was dug in two 20th-century campaigns, 1933–1935 and 1951–1953, roughly two centuries after Pompeii excavations began in the 1700s. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-venus-in-the-shell/)

**Q: What happened to the house in World War II?**
A: Pompeii was hit by Allied bombing in 1943, and this house sustained damage in those raids, part of wider wartime damage across the excavated city. [it.wikipedia.org](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_della_Venere_in_conchiglia)
