## What you see

- The **museum building itself**, a purpose-built site-museum structure (not an ancient Roman building) set into the terrace slope near Porta Marina — its own architectural history (1870s origin, 1943 bomb damage, 2021 rebuild) is as much a display as its contents.
- **Plaster casts of eruption victims**, though the specific casts on view have changed over time: three casts previously shown here were moved to the Imperial Villa during the 2024 closure, while recent additions to the Antiquarium's collection include casts from the **Civita Giuliana villa** excavations on Pompeii's outskirts. Note that the park's largest, most systematic cast display — 22 plaster casts with a dedicated narrative on casting technique and volcanology — opened in **Palestra Grande**, not the Antiquarium, on 12 March 2026, so "the casts" are now split across at least two on-site venues. [pompeiisites.org — Antiquarium page](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/antiquarium/) [pompeiisites.org — Aug 2024 closure notice](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/closure-of-the-antiquarium-and-opening-of-the-imperial-villa-the-house-of-tryptolemus-and-the-house-of-the-sailor/) [pompeiisites.org — Palestra Grande casts exhibition](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/the-plaster-casts-of-pompeii-an-itinerary-through-the-eruption-of-ad-79/)
- The **Moregine silver treasure**, a set of Roman silver tableware found near Moregine (just south of Pompeii), including a piece inscribed with an owner's name, "Erasti sum" ("I belong to Erastus"). [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm) [en.wikipedia.org — Antiquarium of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarium_of_Pompeii)
- **Frescoes from the House of the Golden Bracelet** (Casa del Bracciale d'Oro), one of the site's most cited find-groups, displayed among the museum's fresco holdings. [en.wikipedia.org — Antiquarium of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarium_of_Pompeii)
- A **marble Nike (Victory) statue** from Villa A at Oplontis, and a **bronze Ephebus lamp-holder** from a house in Regio VII (VII.16.17-22), plus a bronze food-warmer from I.8.17 — everyday and luxury bronzework alongside sculpture. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm)
- **Glass cameo (cameo-glass) panel fragments** from the House of Fabius Rufus, and material connected to the House of the Menander, including fresco fragments depicting the wedding of Alexander and Roxane. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm)
- A chronological display narrative running from the **Samnite era (4th century BC)** through to the **AD 79 eruption**, with particular attention to Pompeii's ties to Rome — the museum functions as a compressed "history of the city" rather than a single-theme collection. [pompeiisites.org — 2021 opening notice](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/on-the-25th-january-the-antiquarium-of-pompeii-opens-to-the-public/)

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second version:**
The Antiquarium is Pompeii's own on-site museum, built specifically so that finds from the excavations would not all have to leave for Naples. It has been bombed, closed for decades, and rebuilt — its own troubled history mirrors the city's. Today it holds real plaster casts, silverware, frescoes, and everyday objects that tell Pompeii's story from long before the eruption to the moment it happened.

**(b) ~150-word version:**
The Antiquarium was built by archaeologist Giuseppe Fiorelli in 1873–1874 as a dedicated exhibition venue for finds illustrating daily life at Pompeii, and was expanded in 1926 by director Amedeo Maiuri, who added excavation maps and material from Villa Pisanella and Via dell'Abbondanza. In September 1943, during Allied bombing raids on the Pompeii excavation area in the Second World War, the building suffered extensive damage. It was restored and reopened on 13 June 1948, timed to the bicentenary of Pompeii's excavations. A further blow came from the 1980 Irpinia earthquake, after which the museum stayed shut for roughly 36 years, reopening partially in 2016 as a visitor-centre space and then fully, with an entirely new layout "inspired by Maiuri's original museum philosophy," on **25 January 2021**. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm) [pompeiisites.org — 2021 opening notice](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/on-the-25th-january-the-antiquarium-of-pompeii-opens-to-the-public/) [en.wikipedia.org — Antiquarium of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarium_of_Pompeii)

**(c) ~250-word deep-dive:**
Architecturally the Antiquarium is a modern (19th–21st century) purpose-built museum structure tucked into the terrace below the Temple of Venus, not a repurposed ancient building — a design choice by Fiorelli meant to keep excavated material physically close to its findspots rather than shipping everything to the royal collection in Naples. Sources consulted diverge slightly on the founding date (one source credits 1861 as a conceptual founding, while pompeiisites.org and pompeiiinpictures.com credit the physical building to Fiorelli in 1873–1874 — flagged here as a minor, unresolved discrepancy). The 2021 reopening rebuilt the museum around a chronological narrative — Samnite-era origins (4th century BC) through the Roman colony period to the AD 79 eruption — organized to foreground Pompeii's link to Rome, alongside recent excavation finds. Notable pieces include the Moregine silver table service, frescoes from the House of the Golden Bracelet, sculpture from Oplontis and the House of Menander context, and bronzework from several Regio VII and I houses. On casts specifically: the Antiquarium is not the sole (or even primary) cast venue today — three of its casts moved to the Imperial Villa during an August 2024 refitting closure, and the park's dedicated 22-cast exhibition ("The Plaster Casts of Pompeii") opened separately in Palestra Grande in March 2026, while newer Civita Giuliana-villa casts have been associated with the Antiquarium's own recent additions. Since reopening, the museum has also undergone a further maintenance-driven closure (from 1 August 2024) and, as of July 2026, live 3D-digitisation work on displayed artefacts — meaning the exhibition is a moving target even post-2021, and specific room contents may shift between visits. ## The detail most visitors miss

Among the Moregine silver-tableware display, one piece carries a scratched owner's inscription reading "**Erasti sum**" — "I am [the property] of Erastus" — a personal name etched directly into the metal by its Roman owner, a small, human, ownership-marking detail easy to walk past in a case full of more visually striking objects. The exact room number for this case within the current post-2021 layout is not specified in the sources reviewed, so ask museum staff or check the floor plan on arrival; it is documented as part of the Antiquarium's Moregine silver-treasure holdings. ## Anecdotes

- **A museum built to keep Pompeii's story at Pompeii:** Fiorelli's 1870s Antiquarium was a deliberate counterweight to the practice of shipping the best excavated material off to the royal (later national) collection in Naples — a purpose-built local showcase for finds illustrating everyday life. This is documented institutional history, not legend. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm)
- **A war wound, not a volcanic one:** In September 1943, Allied bombing raids over the Pompeii excavation area during the Second World War badly damaged the Antiquarium building — a reminder that some of the site's scars are 20th-century, not 1st-century. The museum did not reopen until nearly five years later, on 13 June 1948, deliberately timed to coincide with the 200th anniversary of Pompeii's excavations. True, sourced history. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm) [pompeiisites.org — 2021 opening notice](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/on-the-25th-january-the-antiquarium-of-pompeii-opens-to-the-public/)
- **A museum still under construction, decades after reopening:** Even after its celebrated 25 January 2021 relaunch, the Antiquarium closed again on 1 August 2024 for maintenance and exhibition refitting, and by July 2026 was hosting live 3D-digitisation work on its own displayed artefacts — the "finished" museum a visitor sees today is, officially, still a work in progress. True, sourced from official park press releases. ## FAQ

- **Why was the Antiquarium closed for so long?** It was badly damaged by Allied bombing in September 1943 and again by the 1980 Irpinia earthquake; after the earthquake it stayed shut for roughly 36 years, reopening partially in 2016 and fully, with a new layout, on 25 January 2021. It then closed again from August 2024 for further maintenance. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm) [pompeiisites.org — Aug 2024 closure notice](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/closure-of-the-antiquarium-and-opening-of-the-imperial-villa-the-house-of-tryptolemus-and-the-house-of-the-sailor/)
- **Are the plaster casts here real?** Yes — plaster casts displayed at Pompeii (here and elsewhere on-site) are genuine casts poured into the voids left in the hardened volcanic ash by decomposed human and animal bodies, not replicas; the Antiquarium's own holdings include casts connected to the recent Civita Giuliana villa excavations, though its exact cast roster has changed with recent closures and the 2026 Palestra Grande cast exhibition. [pompeiisites.org — Antiquarium page](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/antiquarium/) [pompeiisites.org — Palestra Grande casts exhibition](https://pompeiisites.org/en/comunicati/the-plaster-casts-of-pompeii-an-itinerary-through-the-eruption-of-ad-79/)
- **Is this different from the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN)?** Yes — MANN in Naples holds a large, separate share of Pompeii's portable finds (much of it dating to the historic royal/national collection), while the Antiquarium was specifically built to keep other finds on-site near their findspots. The precise, itemised division of material between the two institutions is not fully documented in the sources reviewed for this file, so treat the general distinction as confirmed but the specifics as only partially sourced. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm)
- **What's the must-see object here?** Coverage from pompeiisites.org and secondary sources most consistently highlights the Moregine silver table-service and the frescoes from the House of the Golden Bracelet as the collection's standout pieces, alongside the site's own plaster casts. [en.wikipedia.org — Antiquarium of Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarium_of_Pompeii) [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R8/8%2001%2004.htm)
