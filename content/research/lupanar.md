# Lupanar (VII.12.18)

## Identity

- **Official name:** English: "Lupanar" (also called "Lupanare Grande" or the "Purpose-Built Brothel"); Italian: "Lupanare di Pompei". "Lupanar" derives from Latin *lupa* ("she-wolf", slang for prostitute). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- **Address:** Regio VII, Insula 12, doorway 18 (catalogued VII.12.18, sometimes given as VII.12.18–20 for the full multi-door structure), at the corner of Vicolo del Lupanare and Vicolo del Balcone Pensile, about two blocks east/south-east of the Forum, near the Stabian Baths and Via Stabiana. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- **GPS:** 40.7503, 14.4868 (decimal degrees; also expressed as 40°45'0.9"N, 14°29'12.4"E). Source: Wikidata item Q157747 ("Lupanar"), cross-checked against the coordinates cited in the English Wikipedia article. Precision is to ~4 decimal places (building footprint, not a single-doorway pinpoint) — treat as **approx**. [Wikidata Q157747](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q157747), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- **Opening hours (official, pompeiisites.org, checked for 2026):** The Archaeological Park runs a two-season schedule: **16 March – 14 October**: 9:00–19:00, last entry 17:30; **15 October – 15 March**: 9:00–17:00, last entry 15:30. Since the stop's visit date is **20 October 2026**, it falls in the **winter schedule**: open 9:00, last entry 15:30, closes 17:00. Daily visitor cap is 20,000, with a nominative (named) ticket requirement, and tickets are sold exclusively via Vivaticket (www.vivaticket.it) as of the 2026 season. No Lupanar-specific closure notice was found for October 2026 at the time of research (checked 11 Sept 2026) — visitors should still check pompeiisites.org's "Timetables and tickets" page shortly before the visit, since the Lupanar has closed for short maintenance periods before (see History). [pompeiisites.org – Timetables and tickets](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/)
- The Lupanar itself, per Pompeii Sites' visitable-buildings listing, has been separately noted with hours of 09:15–18:20 (last entrance 18:00) in some site listings — this appears to be an older/cached figure; the visiting-info timetable above (fetched directly, 2026) is the authoritative current source and should be treated as controlling. ## What you see

- A two-storey brick-and-rubble (opus incertum-type masonry) building on a street corner, with two entrances on Vicolo del Lupanare and a third on Vicolo del Balcone Pensile. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)
- Five small ground-floor cellae (cubicles) arranged along a short central corridor, each just large enough for a **masonry (brick/stone) bed platform** built directly against the wall, originally topped with a mattress. Visible wear/scuff marks are described on the bed masonry. [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/lupanar), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)
- **Erotic frescoes** painted directly onto the plaster above each cubicle doorway — originally eight scenes, seven substantially surviving, showing various heterosexual positions and a Priapus figure; these appear to be **original in-situ wall paintings**, not replicas — no source checked reports they were detached and moved to MANN in Naples (unlike some portable panel paintings from other Pompeian houses that did go to Naples' "Secret Cabinet"). This could not be independently confirmed with a museum-specific source, so treat the "still in situ" status as the consensus of the sources checked rather than a confirmed museum record. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)
- Dense wall **graffiti** scratched into the plaster — over 150 inscriptions transcribed in total (Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum IV series), in Latin, Greek and Oscan, concentrated most heavily near the entrance. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)
- A latrine at the west end of the building, behind a partition, with its own small window. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)
- Small, high barred windows (little natural light), no doors/curtains recorded on the cell openings, giving the cramped, cell-like character described by visitors. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- A now-restored **upper floor** (originally 5 more rooms, reached historically by an external wooden staircase); condition and current public access to the upper floor were not confirmed in the sources checked — treat as "estimated original layout" rather than a confirmed current visitor experience. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- Phallus carvings on the basalt paving stones/building facades in the surrounding streets, believed to have functioned as directional markers pointing toward the brothel. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second version:** This small building on a Pompeii street corner is the only structure in the entire Roman world that scholars universally agree was purpose-built as a brothel. Its walls still carry the original erotic frescoes that once decorated the cubicle doorways, and over 150 pieces of graffiti — names, prices, boasts — scratched by clients and by the enslaved and freed women who worked here. It was buried, and so preserved, by the eruption of Vesuvius in AD 79.

**(b) 2-minute version (~150 words):** The Lupanar served Pompeii's commercial quarter near the Forum and Stabian Baths, catering mainly to merchants, freedmen and enslaved people rather than the elite. Five ground-floor cells, each with a built-in masonry bed, opened onto a short corridor; a similar set of rooms existed on an upper floor reached by an external staircase, possibly serving different clients or providing extra capacity. The plaster above each doorway carried an erotic fresco, and the walls were covered by client and worker graffiti — a coin embedded in the plaster dates one phase of the building to AD 72–79, and it was clearly in active use right up to the eruption. In the AD 62 earthquake that damaged much of Pompeii, evidence for the Lupanar specifically was not found in the sources checked; the standing building visible today reflects its final, pre-eruption state. It was excavated in **1862**. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)

**(c) Deep-dive (~250 words):** The Lupanar was run by a *leno* (brothel-keeper/pimp), who according to one source acquired enslaved women — often from the eastern Mediterranean — for roughly 600 sesterces each; the workforce is generally understood as a mix of enslaved and free/freed women, with one scholarly estimate suggesting about a fifth of the names recorded indicate free status. Names scratched into the walls are overwhelmingly Greek (e.g. Restituta, Spes, Successa, Eutychis, and the male name Menander), consistent with an enslaved or freed population of eastern origin, though this is an inference from naming patterns rather than a documented fact. [Aeon (Levin-Richardson research summary)](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/s.php/pompei-proibita-en-214-s1.htm)

Prices recorded in the graffiti range from **2 to 16 asses**, with 2 asses (roughly the price of a cheap loaf of bread) appearing to be a common low rate — e.g. the well-known inscription "Eutychis, a Greek lass with sweet ways, 2 asses" (a Greek/Latin advertisement-style graffito). [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women), [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/s.php/pompei-proibita-en-214-s1.htm)

Scholars disagree sharply on how many brothels Pompeii actually had. Early 20th-century excavators labelled any building with erotic imagery a brothel, reaching counts as high as **34–35**; classicist Andrew Wallace-Hadrill later applied strict criteria (masonry bed in a small publicly-accessible cell + explicit paintings + a cluster of "hic bene futui"-type graffiti, at least two of the three) and concluded there was only **one certain purpose-built brothel** — the Lupanar — with other single "cellae meretriciae" scattered around the city; Thomas McGinn's competing model counts as many as 26 brothels plus 13 more cellae. The interpretation of the frescoes as a literal "service menu" versus general erotic/atmospheric decoration is likewise disputed among scholars. [Bryn Mawr Classical Review](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2004/2004.04.40/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women)

The building was damaged in WWII-era conditions and given a restoration roughly 57 years before 2006 (i.e., around **1949**, per one Italian-language source); it then underwent a **one-year, $253,000 restoration** of its frescoes and structure, reopening **26 October 2006**. It closed again for maintenance and reopened **19 July 2021**, reported as one of the most-requested buildings by visitors. Both restorations were part of wider EU-funded Pompeii conservation programmes (the "Grande Progetto Pompei", concluded 2023). No specific 2022–2026 conservation project targeting the Lupanar itself was found in the sources checked. [portanapoli.com](https://www.portanapoli.com/Ita/Pompei/lupanare.html), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna15434770), [Artribune](https://www.artribune.com/arti-visive/archeologia-arte-antica/2021/07/pompei-villa-misteri-lupanare/), [eunews.it](https://www.eunews.it/2023/10/04/ue-successo-programma-restauro-pompei/)

## The detail most visitors miss

A **tabella ansata** (an inscribed plaque with dovetail-shaped handles at each end) near the entrance restricts visits to "not more than 10 persons at a time" — look for this small inscribed sign as you enter; it's an actual ancient/site-management notice about crowd control at the doorway, easy to walk past while looking up at the frescoes. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R7/7%2012%2018%20p2.htm)

## Anecdotes

- A coin impressed into fresh plaster during a building phase has been dated to AD 72, giving archaeologists a fixed point showing the structure was still being worked on/refreshed only 7 years before the eruption. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- One of the most quoted graffiti reads (Latin) *"Hic ego puellas multas futui"* — "Here I f***ed many girls" — a boastful client inscription, one of dozens of similar first-person boasts scratched into the corridor walls. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- The advertisement-style graffito naming "Eutychis, a Greek lass with sweet ways, 2 asses" is treated by researchers as a rare case of a worker (or someone on her behalf) advertising her own price and manner directly on the wall — one of the clearest windows historians have onto the economic reality of the women who worked there. [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women)
- Note on a widely repeated claim: some popular articles state the Lupanar "catered mainly to traders/merchants visiting the commercial town," implying a very specific clientele profile — this is a reasonable inference from the location (a busy commercial corner) but is presented by tourist-facing sources as more certain than the academic sources checked would support; treat as **plausible but not firmly documented**. [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/s.php/pompei-proibita-en-214-s1.htm)

## FAQ

- **Is the Lupanar the only brothel in Pompeii?** It is the only structure universally agreed by scholars to have been purpose-built as a brothel; researchers disagree on how many additional single-room "cellae meretriciae" existed elsewhere in the city, with estimates ranging from one (Wallace-Hadrill's strict criteria) up to several dozen under looser older criteria. [Bryn Mawr Classical Review](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2004/2004.04.40/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
- **Were the workers enslaved?** Most were likely enslaved, based on Greek names and the *leno* (brothel-keeper) system of ownership; a minority of names in the graffiti are interpreted as indicating free or freed status — exact proportions are estimated, not census-documented. [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women)
- **How much did it cost?** Graffiti record prices from 2 to 16 asses, with 2 asses (about the price of a cheap loaf of bread) as a common low rate. [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/what-pompeiis-ruins-say-about-its-enslaved-prostituted-women), [pompeii.org.uk](https://www.pompeii.org.uk/s.php/pompei-proibita-en-214-s1.htm)
- **When was it excavated and can I still visit in October 2026?** It was excavated in 1862 and remains open to visitors; for 20 October 2026 specifically, Pompeii's official winter hours apply (9:00–17:00, last entry 15:30) per pompeiisites.org, with no Lupanar-specific closure found as of this research. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/)
