# Thermopolium (Stop 11) — VI.8.9, Via delle Terme

> **Route note:** This stop is the thermopolium at **VI.8.9**, Regio VI, Insula 8, on Via delle Terme, directly opposite the Forum Baths (Terme del Foro). It is identified below as the best-fit tour stop "between the House of the Vettii and the Lupanar" — see reasoning in Identity. The **famous Regio V thermopolium** (excavated December 2020) is a **different building in a different Regio** and is covered only as background/comparison context, clearly labeled throughout.

## Identity

- **Route stop — official identification:** The thermopolium at **VI.8.9** (Regio VI, Insula 8, doorway 9), fronting **Via delle Terme**, directly facing the **Forum Baths (Terme del Foro)** across the street. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- **Why this one, not another Regio VI/VII thermopolium:** The stop brief specifies a Regio VI thermopolium on or near Via Consolare/Via delle Terme, on the walking route between the House of the Vettii (VI.15.1, near the Vesuvian Gate, north end of Regio VI) and the Lupanar (VII.12.18–20, two blocks east of the Forum). VI.8.9 sits exactly on Via delle Terme, at the point where that street meets Via Consolare, roughly midway on a plausible south-bound walking route from the Vettii down through Regio VI toward the Forum and onward to Via Stabiana/Vicolo del Lupanare. [pompeiiinpictures.com — Via delle Terme](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Streets/Via_delle_Terme.htm), [Wikipedia — House of the Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii), [Wikipedia — Lupanar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupanar)
  Other candidates considered and set aside: VI.16.2 (a thermopolium further east, less directly on the Vettii–Lupanar axis) and VI.1.18 ("thermopolium and popina of Phoebus," near the Herculaneum Gate, north-west of the Vettii — off-route in the wrong direction). VI.8.9's position facing the Forum Baths on Via delle Terme is the strongest geographic match to the brief. Confidence: **moderate-high** — the street/building match is exact, but no single source explicitly labels VI.8.9 as "the standard stop between Vettii and Lupanar," so this is a reasoned geographic identification rather than a confirmed official tour-route citation. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- **Comparison building (background only, NOT this stop):** The famous "Thermopolium of Regio V" is at **V.3** (Regio V, Insula 3), at the intersection of Vicolo delle Nozze d'Argento and Vicolo dei Balconi — a different Regio, roughly on the opposite (eastern/north-eastern) side of the excavated city from Regio VI. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [pompeiisites.org press release](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/)

## What you see

**At the route stop, VI.8.9 (Via delle Terme):**

- A street-front **masonry counter** (banco) facing directly onto Via delle Terme, finished with a **marble facing** on the counter top/front. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- **Four dolia** (large storage jars) still embedded and intact against the east wall, in the north-east corner of the counter — used to hold dry goods, food, or drink for service. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- A **maker's stamp** on one of the dolia reading "from the workshop of A(ulus) Appuleius Hilario" — physical evidence of the commercial supply chain behind the shop's equipment. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- A **hearth** built into the rear of the counter, for warming food/drink. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- Behind the counter, a **larger rear room reached by two steps up**, connecting onward to smaller side chambers (one to the east, others to the north) and, via a barred doorway, to the neighbouring property VI.8.13. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- A **downpipe** in the north-west corner of the rear room, part of the building's water-management system. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- Directly across the street: the entrance front of the **Forum Baths**, giving the visitor a clear sense of the shop's customer base (bathers leaving the Terme del Foro). [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- The building was excavated across three separate campaigns — **1823, 1826, and 1845** — typical of Pompeii's long, piecemeal 19th-century excavation history. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)

**Background comparison ONLY — the Regio V house (V.3), NOT this stop:**

- The counter carries a painted **Nereid riding a sea-horse/hippocampus**, shown holding a lyre with two dolphins swimming alongside — the best-known image from the 2020 excavation. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)
- Painted **still-life "menu" imagery**: two mallard ducks shown upside down (ready for cooking), a rooster, and other food animals — understood as advertising for dishes actually sold there. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/)
- A painted **dog on a leash**, widely interpreted (though not certainly) as a reminder to customers to keep their own dogs restrained. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)
- A smaller image on the short end of the counter, thought to be a **shop "trademark"/sign** for the business itself. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)
- **Dolia count is reported inconsistently across sources**: most press coverage of the 2020 discovery cites **eight** dolia in the counter; pompeiiinpictures.com's building-by-building description counts **ten** dolia in the same structure. Flagged as **disputed/uncertain** — not resolved by the sources checked. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium)
- **What was found inside the dolia:** fragments of duck, pig (swine), goat, fish, and land snails, plus evidence of broken/crushed beans (possibly used to adjust wine's colour or flavour) and residue consistent with wine storage — a set of findings broadly consistent with the food items depicted on the painted counter. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium)
- **Human and animal remains near the counter:** a mature/senescent individual (an estimated 50+ years old) found near what appeared to be a wooden bed (nail and wood residue recorded), and bones of at least one further individual found inside one of the large dolia; a **complete small-dog skeleton** (roughly 20–25 cm at the shoulder) was found in the north-west corner between two doorways — evidence cited as suggesting selective dog breeding in Roman Pompeii. **Note:** this is distinct from the officially publicised finding, at the same August 2021 public-opening event, of roughly ten skeletal remains subjected to DNA analysis in the nearby "House with the Garden" — a separate building in the same excavation area. Do not conflate the two; sources checked describe them separately, and the human-remains-inside-the-thermopolium detail comes only from pompeiiinpictures.com's building notes, not from the official pompeiisites.org press release, so it should be treated as **less firmly confirmed** than the frescoes/dolia contents. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [pompeiisites.org press release](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/)
- **MANN (Naples Archaeological Museum):** no source checked reports that any fixtures, frescoes, or finds from either VI.8.9 or the Regio V thermopolium have been relocated to MANN. The counter structures and frescoes remain in situ at both sites in all sources consulted; this is a negative finding (absence of evidence), not a confirmed "nothing ever moved."

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second version:** A thermopolium was a Roman street-front counter selling ready-to-eat hot food and drink — the ancient equivalent of a fast-food stall — mainly serving Pompeii's lower- and middle-class residents, many of whom lived in apartments without their own kitchens. This one, at VI.8.9 on Via delle Terme, stood right across the street from the Forum Baths, so its regular customers would have included bathers heading in or out of the Terme del Foro. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium), [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)

**(b) 2-minute version (~150 words):** Pompeii has yielded somewhere on the order of **80 thermopolia** (a figure attributed to former Park director Massimo Osanna), though other sources cite as many as **89**; the exact count varies by methodology and is best treated as an estimate in the 80–90 range. These counters were a basic fixture of Roman street life: cheap, fast, and public, they let city-dwellers without home kitchens buy hot food and wine on the way past. VI.8.9 itself was uncovered piecemeal across three 19th-century campaigns — 1823, 1826, and 1845 — long before modern excavation and conservation standards existed, which is typical of much of Regio VI's early exposure. Like the rest of Pompeii, the shop was buried and sealed by the eruption of Vesuvius on (most recent scholarship favours) 24 October AD 79, preserving its counter, dolia, and hearth largely as the last owner left them. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium), [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)

**(c) Deep-dive (~250 words):** Archaeologists identify and date thermopolia primarily by their distinctive L- or straight-shaped masonry counter with embedded dolia opening onto the street — a form that recurs across dozens of Pompeian blocks and is rarely used for anything else. Contents analysis of the dolia (where preserved, as at the Regio V example) has recovered animal bone fragments (duck, pig, goat, fish) and plant remains (beans), pointing to prepared-food and flavoured-wine service rather than simple raw-goods storage. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)

A live scholarly debate concerns both the name and the function of these shops. Archaeologist Steven J. R. Ellis (University of Cincinnati; later University of Sydney), along with scholars including Tönnes Kleberg, Mary Beard, and Claire Holleran, has argued that "thermopolium" (from Greek *thermos*, "hot," + *poleo*, "to sell") was rarely the term Romans themselves used — the more common ancient term in the literary record is *popina* — and that modern assumptions the counters were mainly for "hot" fast food are not strongly supported by the archaeological evidence itself; Ellis's Pompeii Archaeological Research Project (Porta Stabia) work emphasises building the picture from excavated evidence (burning residue, food remains, structural traces) rather than from literary labels alone. [Wikipedia — Steven J. R. Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_J._R._Ellis)

The Regio V thermopolium (V.3) was partially exposed in 2019 during Great Pompeii Project stabilisation work on unexcavated fronts, then fully excavated across 2019–December 2020 under the scientific direction of Massimo Osanna, then Director General of the Archaeological Park (later Director General of Museums). It opened to the public on **12 August 2021**. [pompeiisites.org press release](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)

## The detail most visitors miss

At VI.8.9, look closely at the rim of the **north-east corner dolium** (against the east wall): it carries the **maker's stamp of the workshop of Aulus Appuleius Hilario**. Most visitors' eyes go straight to the counter shape and the view of the Forum Baths across the street, and walk past this small stamped inscription — physical proof that even a humble street-food jar came from a named commercial pottery workshop with its own brand identity, two thousand years before the concept of a "supplier." [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)

## Anecdotes

- The reveal of the fully excavated Regio V thermopolium in December 2020 became a global media story largely because of its vivid, well-preserved paint — the swimming Nereid, the ducks, the leashed dog — giving journalists a single, photogenic "ancient menu" image; Massimo Osanna described the completed excavation as offering "a unique opportunity for interdisciplinary research" into Pompeian diet and daily life. This is the discovery visitors usually mean when they ask about "the thermopolium in the news" — worth flagging clearly to your group that it is a different Regio from the stop you are standing at. [pompeiisites.org press release](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/)
- The scholarly disagreement over "thermopolium" versus "popina" is a genuine, ongoing debate among specialists (Ellis, Beard, Holleran, Kleberg), not settled trivia — worth flagging to visitors as a live academic argument about Roman commercial vocabulary rather than a fixed fact. **Flagged as disputed.** [Wikipedia — Steven J. R. Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_J._R._Ellis)
- Sources disagree even on how many dolia the famous Regio V counter has (eight vs. ten) — a small but genuine reminder that "settled" archaeological facts about even a recently and carefully excavated site can still vary between the press-release account and the detailed building-by-building archive. **Flagged as disputed/uncertain.** [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm), [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium)

## FAQ

- **Is this the famous thermopolium from the news?** No. The one in the news (excavated and revealed December 2020, opened to the public 12 August 2021) is in **Regio V**, a different part of the site; this stop is in **Regio VI** on Via delle Terme, facing the Forum Baths. Both are genuine ancient snack-bar counters, but they are different buildings. [pompeiisites.org press release](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/), [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2008%2009.htm)
- **What did Romans actually eat/drink at a counter like this?** Archaeological residue evidence (mainly from the Regio V dolia) points to duck, pig, goat, fish, and land snails, plus wine, sometimes adjusted with crushed beans; VI.8.9's hearth suggests food or drink was also warmed on-site. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium), [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R5/5%2003%20nereid.htm)
- **Why didn't most Pompeians cook at home?** Many people in Pompeii's denser housing (upper-floor apartments and smaller units) lacked a private kitchen or hearth, making street counters like this a practical, everyday food source rather than an occasional treat. [English Wikipedia — Thermopolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopolium)
- **Does "thermopolium" mean it definitely served hot food?** Not necessarily — the Greek-derived name suggests "hot," but modern scholars (Ellis, Beard, Holleran, Kleberg) note the ancient term actually used in Latin texts was more often *popina*, and caution against assuming every counter was primarily a hot-food bar; this remains an active scholarly debate. [Wikipedia — Steven J. R. Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_J._R._Ellis)

## Hebrew naming

- Thermopolium → **תרמופוליום** — a direct phonetic transliteration of the Latin/Greek-derived term; this is the natural and expected Hebrew rendering (parallel to other Greek/Latin loanwords already standard in Hebrew archaeological writing, e.g. אמפיתיאטרון, מוזאיקה). No Hebrew-language tour-guide source was checked to confirm this is the term guides use in the field (search budget for this task did not extend to Hebrew-language sources) — treat as a **recommended transliteration**, not a confirmed field-usage citation.
- Dolium (singular) / dolia (plural) → **דוליום / דוליה** (transliteration); if a plain-language gloss is wanted for a general audience, **כדי אחסון גדולים מחרס** ("large ceramic storage jars") works well spoken aloud.
- Banco / counter → **דלפק** (standard Hebrew word for "counter," works naturally for the masonry service counter).
- "Fast food" (as a spoken analogy for visitors) → keep as the loanword **פסט פוד** (Israeli guides commonly reach for this English loanword rather than a literal Hebrew coinage when explaining thermopolia).
- Regio VI → **רג'יו 6** (transliterate "Regio" as ר'ג'יו, consistent with usage elsewhere in this project, e.g. the Lupanar file's "רג'יו 7").
