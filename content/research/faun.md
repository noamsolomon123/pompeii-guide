# House of the Faun (id=9)

## Identity

## What you see

- **The Dancing Faun (bronze statue) — this is a COPY.** The original bronze statuette (MANN inventory no. 5002) was found in late October 1830 near the impluvium of the main atrium; sources differ on the exact day (26 October per Wikipedia's account, 28 October per pompeiiinpictures.com) — treat the precise day as **uncertain**. The original is displayed at the Naples National Archaeological Museum (MANN); the figure standing in the atrium impluvium today at Pompeii is a modern reproduction. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun); [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p2.htm)
- **The Alexander Mosaic — this is also a COPY in situ.** The original floor mosaic (5.82–5.83 m wide × 3.13 m tall, roughly 1.5–2 million tesserae depending on the counting method) was found in the exedra between the two peristyles and depicts the Battle of Issus (333 BC) between Alexander the Great and Darius III. The original was lifted from the floor and taken to Naples in 1844; it was initially set into the museum floor (where visitors walked on it) and only remounted on a wall in 1916, where it remains, at MANN. [thehistoryblog.com/archives/60419](http://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/60419); [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mosaic) A high-quality reproduction occupies the original floor position at Pompeii today. [thepompeii.com](https://thepompeii.com/what-to-see/house-of-the-faun/) **Caveat:** as of the restoration project running 2020 onward, the original mosaic at MANN has at times been off public view for conservation work, with visitors able to watch the restorers through viewing points; confirm current display status before the visit. [museoarcheologiconapoli.it/en/the-restoration-of-the-mosaic-of-alexander](https://www.museoarcheologiconapoli.it/en/the-restoration-of-the-mosaic-of-alexander/)
- **Two atria.** A main Tuscan-style atrium (housing the faun's impluvium) and a second, smaller "tetrastyle" (four-column) atrium — an unusually lavish doubling that signals the owner's wealth. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun)
- **Two peristyle gardens.** A first (inner) peristyle and a larger rear peristyle described as surrounded by 44 columns, occupying roughly the rear half of the property. [pompeiicity.com/en/places/house-of-the-faun](https://pompeiicity.com/en/places/house-of-the-faun)
- **The "HAVE" inscription.** A floor mosaic of colored tesserae at the entrance/vestibule spells out "HAVE" — Latin for "Hail!" / "Welcome!" — a stock formula also found in other Pompeian doorways. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun); [pompeiiandvesuvius.wordpress.com](https://pompeiiandvesuvius.wordpress.com/2018/11/16/mosaics-in-the-house-of-the-faun-that-arent-the-alexander-mosaic/)
- **Scale of the house:** approximately 3,000 m² (English Wikipedia/Pompeii-city sources round to ~3,000 m²; the Italian Wikipedia gives a more precise 2,970 m² for the layout following the late-2nd-century BC reconstruction). It occupies essentially the whole of Insula 12, making it the largest private residence in Pompeii. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno)
- **Other mosaics (originals removed to MANN; some findspots visible in situ):** a Nilotic (Nile River) frieze on the exedra walls showing a hippopotamus, crocodile, snake and various birds; a "cat with partridge, ducks, fish and shellfish" emblema found in an ala off the first atrium (found 1 December 1830, MANN inv. 9993); a doves-extracting-a-necklace-from-a-jewelry-box emblema from the opposite ala (also found 1 December 1830); and a tragic theatrical mask mosaic at the inner end of the entrance corridor (fauces). [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p5.htm); [pompeiiandvesuvius.wordpress.com](https://pompeiiandvesuvius.wordpress.com/2018/11/16/mosaics-in-the-house-of-the-faun-that-arent-the-alexander-mosaic/)
- **First Style ("incrustation") stucco/fresco decoration** appears throughout the house — painted stucco imitating marble veneer panels — rather than the later, more figurative styles seen elsewhere in Pompeii; much of this was lost to 1943 bombing damage on the atrium's north and east walls (see History). [pompeiicity.com](https://pompeiicity.com/en/places/house-of-the-faun); [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p5.htm)

## History in layers

### (a) 30-second intro
The House of the Faun is the largest private home ever excavated at Pompeii — an entire city block built by one of the city's wealthiest families. It is famous for two masterpieces found inside it: a bronze Dancing Faun statuette and the Alexander Mosaic, a vast floor picture of Alexander the Great in battle. Both originals now live in the Naples Archaeological Museum; what you see on-site are careful reproductions in their original findspots.

### (b) ~150 words, 2-minute story
The house began in the 3rd century BC as a smaller residence, then was rebuilt and enlarged around 120 BC into the double-atrium, double-peristyle mansion that survives today, eventually covering roughly 3,000 m² — almost the whole of Insula 12. [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno) Ownership is not proven by any single document, but inscriptions found in the house — an Oscan text naming a Satrius who held the office of aedile, and a Latin inscription with the cognomen Saturninus — point to the Satria family, with a ring bearing the name Cassius suggesting a later marriage tie to the gens Cassia. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun) The house takes its name from the bronze faun statuette found in the atrium's basin in 1830. Like the rest of Pompeii, it was damaged in the AD 62 earthquake and repaired, then buried by the AD 79 eruption of Vesuvius. Excavation began in 1830–1831, when the Dancing Faun and Alexander Mosaic were both discovered.

### (c) ~250 words deep-dive
Because no document names an owner outright, scholarship on the house has built a case from indirect evidence: an Oscan-language inscription referencing a Satrius serving as aedile, a Latin graffito with the cognomen "Saturninus," and a signet ring inscribed with the name Cassius — together suggested to indicate ownership by the Samnite-era Satria family, possibly later intermarried with the Cassii. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun) No scholarly consensus definitively identifies the owner, and this remains an active debate rather than a settled fact.

The decoration is classified as Pompeian **First Style** ("incrustation" or "masonry" style): painted stucco relief made to resemble cut marble veneer panels, the earliest and most austere of the four canonical Pompeian wall-painting styles, consistent with the house's 2nd-century-BC core. [pompeiicity.com](https://pompeiicity.com/en/places/house-of-the-faun)

The Alexander Mosaic (dated by most scholars to roughly 120–100 BC) is widely thought to be a Roman copy of a lost Hellenistic Greek painting from the late 4th or early 3rd century BC. Ancient testimony from Pliny the Elder describes a battle painting of Alexander and Darius by **Philoxenus of Eretria**, made for the Macedonian king Cassander — the leading (but not universally accepted) candidate for the mosaic's ultimate source; other scholars have proposed Apelles or Aristides of Thebes instead. The attribution remains debated. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mosaic); [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philoxenus_of_Eretria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philoxenus_of_Eretria)

Recent science: a non-invasive 2020s conservation study (published in PLOS One, 2025) analyzed the roughly two million tesserae with portable X-ray fluorescence, FTIR, Raman spectroscopy and multispectral imaging, proposing geological origins in Italy (including the Apuan Alps), the Iberian Peninsula, Greece and Tunisia for the stone tesserae — evidence of an empire-wide material supply chain for the mosaic. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11734927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11734927/); [archaeology.org/news/2025/01/22/restoration-of-the-alexander-mosaic-reveals-tile-sources](https://archaeology.org/news/2025/01/22/restoration-of-the-alexander-mosaic-reveals-tile-sources/)

## The detail most visitors miss

Most visitors walk straight through the first atrium to the Alexander Mosaic exedra, barely glancing at the two small mosaic emblemata set into the floors of the side rooms (alae) flanking that first atrium. Look for the **cat pouncing on a partridge (with ducks, fish and shellfish below)** in the ala to one side, and the **three doves lifting a pearl necklace from a jewelry casket** in the ala on the other — both found on the same day, 1 December 1830, and both of exceptional Hellenistic-style quality, yet routinely bypassed by groups heading straight for the Alexander Mosaic exedra. [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p5.htm)

## Anecdotes

1. **The mosaic that visitors used to walk on.** After being lifted from the Pompeii floor and transported to Naples' Royal Bourbon Museum by oxcart in 1844, the Alexander Mosaic was initially set into the museum's own floor — meaning 19th-century visitors literally walked across the masterpiece for decades — before conservators finally remounted it upright on a wall in 1916, where it has remained since. [thehistoryblog.com/archives/60419](http://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/60419)
2. **A faun that may actually be a satyr.** The Italian Wikipedia entry on the house notes that the bronze statuette which gave the house its name is described by some scholars as depicting a satyr ("raffigurante un satiro"), only conventionally — and, per this account, "erroneously" ("erroneamente interpretato come un fauno") — labeled a "faun" in the 19th-century excavation reports that stuck as the house's name. **This nomenclature point is disputed / not universally repeated** in English-language sources (most, including English Wikipedia, simply call it the Dancing Faun without qualification), so present it as a scholarly aside rather than settled fact. [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno)
3. **Destroyed twice in the 20th century, and rebuilt twice.** The house's secondary (tetrastyle) atrium columns were destroyed by Allied bombing in 1943 — along with three rooms northeast of that atrium and their First Style paintings — then rebuilt after the war, only to be damaged again by the 1980 Irpinia earthquake. A further, technically complex restoration (removing old post-war cement reinforcements and reconsolidating the columns with lime grouts) was completed and reported by the Pompeii Archaeological Park in January 2021. [pompeiisites.org/en/gallery-pompei-en/restoration-columns-house-of-faun](https://pompeiisites.org/en/gallery-pompei-en/restoration-columns-house-of-faun/); [pompeiiinpictures.com](http://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p5.htm)

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Alexander Mosaic I'm looking at the real one?**
No. It is a faithful reproduction set into the original findspot in the exedra. The original is in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN), and has itself periodically been off view during ongoing conservation work since 2020. [thepompeii.com](https://thepompeii.com/what-to-see/house-of-the-faun/); [museoarcheologiconapoli.it](https://www.museoarcheologiconapoli.it/en/the-restoration-of-the-mosaic-of-alexander/)

**Q: Is the bronze Dancing Faun statue original?**
No. The bronze in the atrium impluvium today is a modern copy; the original (MANN inv. 5002) is displayed at the Naples Archaeological Museum. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2012%2002%20p2.htm)

**Q: How big is the House of the Faun, really?**
About 3,000 m², occupying almost the entirety of Insula 12 — making it the largest known private house in Pompeii. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno)

**Q: Why is it called the House of the Faun?**
Because of the bronze statuette found in the main atrium's basin (impluvium) in late October 1830, conventionally described as a "dancing faun" — though at least one source flags this identification (faun vs. satyr) as imprecise. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Faun); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_del_Fauno)

## Hebrew naming

- **House of the Faun / Casa del Fauno →** בֵּית הַפָּאוּן (Beit HaPa'un) — the standard descriptive Hebrew rendering used in guide material; the Italian "Casa del Fauno" is occasionally kept as כָּאזָה דֵל פָאוּנוֹ in more academic/Italian-flavored phrasing, but "בית הפאון" is the natural tour-guide form.
- **Faun →** פָאוּן (transliteration of "faun"; note the statue is technically closer to a satyr — סָאטִיר — in some scholarly readings, worth a one-line aside for guides, per the disputed point above).
- **Atrium →** אַטְרִיּוּם
- **Impluvium →** אִימְפְּלוּבְיוּם (the rainwater basin in the atrium floor)
- **Peristyle →** פֶּרִיסְטִיל (as specified)
- **Mosaic →** פְּסֵיפָס
- **Exedra →** אֶקְסֶדְרָה
- **Tablinum →** טָבְּלִינוּם
- **Insula (city block) →** אִינְסוּלָה
- **Regio (district) →** רֶגְיוֹ / רִיג'וֹ (both transliterations are seen; רג'יו is closer to Italian pronunciation)
- **Fauces (entrance corridor) →** פָאוּצֶס (rare in casual guiding; often just described as "מסדרון הכניסה")

Note: this section reflects standard Hebrew transliteration conventions for Latin/Italian archaeological terms; it was not independently verified against a specific published Israeli tour-guide glossary, so guides should adjust to their own house style/convention where one already exists.
