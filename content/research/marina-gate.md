## What you see

- A **fortified gate tower** straddling the city wall at its western, sea-facing point, built into the slope so it reads as a bastion set on the edge of the plateau. [it.wikipedia summary via search](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompei_(citt%C3%A0_antica))
- **Two parallel barrel-vaulted passages** under one covering vault: a wider, less-steeply-inclined passage (used by pack animals, carts and wagons) and a narrower, steeper stepped passage (for pedestrians), each originally closed by its own gate/doors. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm) [Touring Club Italiano](https://www.touringclub.it/destinazioni/pompei/vedere/209055-scavi-di-pompei-porta-marina)
- **Mixed masonry fabric**: sections of opus incertum (irregular small-stone facing) laid over older courses that include a visible travertine/tuff block, evidence of at least one earlier rebuilding phase in this exact stretch of wall. [pompeiiinpictures.com (walls)](http://pompeiiinpictures.info/pompeiiinpictures/Walls/walls%20p1%20porta%20marina%20to%20sarno%20baths.htm)
- The pedestrian tunnel retains **patches of painted plaster (intonaco)** on its walls and on pilasters flanking the passage, plus painted surfaces above stone benches set along the north side of the tunnel — look up and to the side walls, not just straight ahead. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)
- An **exterior niche** in the outer gate wall that once held a statue of Minerva; only fragments of the statue were recovered on excavation. The present whereabouts/display status of those fragments (MANN Naples vs. in situ vs. stores) is **not confirmed** by the sources consulted for this file — flagged here as unverified rather than stated as fact. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)
- A carved stone **cippus (boundary marker)** near the gate, inscribed with the name and authority of the tribune Titus Suedius Clemens, acting under Emperor Vespasian, ordering the restitution of public land to the city — a rare, precisely dated (c. AD 70s) administrative inscription visible in situ. [vici.org](https://vici.org/vici/17935/)
- Along the wall face, **visible weathering/repair breaks**: further north along the circuit, excavated stretches show a staggering of the top three courses of stone by roughly 10 cm relative to the courses beneath — a repair/impact signature associated by researchers with either the AD 62 earthquake or the force of the AD 79 pyroclastic flows (debated, see below). [ScienceDirect summary via search](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1296207419303346)
- Traces of the **1943 Allied bombing** of the Pompeii excavation area are documented in this zone through period photographs (visible damage recorded in 1944), a reminder the site's modern condition is not purely ancient. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second spoken version:**
Porta Marina is the grandest of Pompeii's city gates, cut into the walls where the ground drops toward what was once the harbour below. Its two stone passages — one for pack animals and carts, one stepped for people on foot — have carried traffic since at least the last centuries BC. Today it is the busiest way into the ruins, still doing the job it always did: bringing visitors up from the sea into the city.

**(b) ~150-word version:**
Pompeii's roughly 3.25 km circuit of walls was built in phases starting as early as the 6th century BC, with a key limestone-faced rebuilding around the 5th century BC and a reinforced Samnite-period phase from the early 3rd century BC onward. [it.wikipedia.org (city)](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompei_(citt%C3%A0_antica)) [Wikipedia EN (Pompeii)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) Porta Marina, the westernmost and most imposing of the city's seven (possibly eight) gates, was built into this fortified stretch to link the hilltop town with its harbour on the Sarno estuary, its two vaulted passages splitting animal and cart traffic from pedestrians. [Touring Club Italiano](https://www.touringclub.it/destinazioni/pompei/vedere/209055-scavi-di-pompei-porta-marina) The city sustained major structural damage in the earthquake of AD 62, prompting years of repair and, near this gate, an imperial land-restitution order under Vespasian recorded on the Suedius Clemens cippus. [vici.org](https://vici.org/vici/17935/) During the AD 79 eruption, the area was later struck by the eruption's pyroclastic surges. Excavation of this specific gate began on 5 March 1861 under Giuseppe Fiorelli, though the structure had first been glimpsed in 1844. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)

**(c) ~250-word deep-dive:**
Administratively, Porta Marina sits at the seam between Regio VII and Regio VIII in the modern regio/insula system used to catalogue Pompeii, since the fortification terrace and the buildings built against it just south of the gate (the Temple of Venus precinct, the Suburban Baths, and the later Villa Imperiale, VIII.1.a) are numbered in Regio VIII. [pompeiiinpictures.com (walls)](http://pompeiiinpictures.info/pompeiiinpictures/Walls/walls%20p1%20porta%20marina%20to%20sarno%20baths.htm) The walls themselves show at least three identifiable building phases — an early phase in soft pappamonte lava (c. early 6th century BC), a "double-façade" limestone (orthostat) rebuild around the late 5th century BC roughly 4 m high with an earthen fill, and a later single-wall limestone/tuff reinforcement from the late 4th–3rd century BC that forms the basis of what is visible today — and scholars continue to debate whether the earliest builders were Oscans, Etruscans, or Samnites. [imperiumromanum.pl](https://imperiumromanum.pl/en/article/city-walls-a-lesser-known-face-of-pompeii/) [Wikipedia EN (Pompeii)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) The gate's own dating and its two-passage design are not seriously disputed in the sources reviewed, but the wall's overall construction chronology and attribution remain an active scholarly question, and it is disputed whether specific damage patterns nearby (e.g., a staggered top course of stone found further along the north walls) date to the AD 62 earthquake or to the force of the AD 79 pyroclastic flows. [ScienceDirect (paywalled abstract)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1296207419303346) On recent work: no pompeiisites.org press release specifically documenting a 2020–2026 restoration campaign at Porta Marina itself was located during this research; the most recent documented change to public access in this area is the reopening of the "Porta Marina Superiore" visitor entrance on 7 June 2021 after an eight-month COVID-19 closure. [pompeitickets.com](https://www.pompeitickets.com/en/reopening-of-the-porta-marina-superiore-entrance) The gate area also suffered damage from Allied bombing of the excavations in 1943, documented in photographs from 1944, with conservation work recorded intermittently since. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)

## The detail most visitors miss

Inside the **pedestrian (northern/left-hand) tunnel**, above the stone benches set along its north wall, patches of ancient painted plaster survive on both the wall surface and the pilasters framing the passage — most visitors walk straight through looking only at the arch ahead and miss this painted decoration at eye level and slightly above, on the tunnel's inner north side. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)

## Anecdotes

- **The land-grab cleanup, c. AD 70–75:** After the AD 62 earthquake, private citizens encroached on public land around the city. Emperor Vespasian sent the tribune Titus Suedius Clemens to investigate and reverse these illegal occupations; his cippus near Porta Marina records, in Latin, that he acted "by the authority of Imperator Caesar Vespasianus Augustus" to restore public land to the city of Pompeii after examining the legal documents and measurements. This is a verified epigraphic record, not legend. [vici.org](https://vici.org/vici/17935/)
- **A gate rediscovered twice:** Porta Marina was first glimpsed by excavators in 1844 but not properly defined and understood until Giuseppe Fiorelli's team began systematic work there on 5 March 1861 — meaning the gate that greets a huge share of today's visitors was only fully revealed roughly 1,782 years after it was buried. This is documented excavation history, not legend. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)
- **A 20th-century wound on an ancient wall:** The Porta Marina area was damaged by Allied bombing in 1943 during the Second World War, with the effects still visible in photographs taken in 1944 — a reminder that some of what looks purely "ancient" damage at the site actually dates from wartime, not from the eruption. Verified from site-documentation photography, not legend. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)

## FAQ

- **Why is it called "Marina" (sea gate) if the sea isn't visible from here today?** Because in antiquity, before the AD 79 eruption reshaped the coastline, the sea and Pompeii's harbour (near the mouth of the Sarno River) were much closer to the city, and this gate's road led directly down to that harbour — the coastline has since retreated due to eruption deposits and geological change. [Touring Club Italiano](https://www.touringclub.it/destinazioni/pompei/vedere/209055-scavi-di-pompei-porta-marina)
- **Why does the gate have two separate passages?** One wider, less steeply inclined passage carried pack animals, carts and wagons, while a second, narrower and steeper stepped passage was reserved for pedestrians — a practical separation of traffic through a single fortified, vaulted structure. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/Gates/Gate%20Marine.htm)
- **How old is Porta Marina?** The surrounding city walls have building phases reaching back to around the 6th century BC, with major limestone-faced rebuilding around the 5th century BC and a reinforced phase from the early 3rd century BC; the gate as visible today belongs to this long, multi-phase fortification system, further modified after Pompeii became a Roman colony around 80 BC. Precise dating of the gate structure itself (as opposed to the wall circuit generally) is not fully settled in the sources reviewed. [imperiumromanum.pl](https://imperiumromanum.pl/en/article/city-walls-a-lesser-known-face-of-pompeii/) [Wikipedia EN (Pompeii)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii)
- **Is Porta Marina the "main" entrance to Pompeii?** It is not necessarily the ancient main gate, but it is today the most-used entrance for modern visitors, being close to the Circumvesuviana train stop "Pompei Scavi – Villa dei Misteri" and serving as one of the park's three official entry points. [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/how-to-get-there/)

## Hebrew naming

- **פּוֹרְטָה מָרִינָה** (Porta Marina) — direct transliteration; this is the form commonly used by Israeli tour guides and Hebrew-language travel content when referring to this specific gate, since "Porta Marina" functions as a proper name rather than a translated term.
- Alternative literal gloss sometimes used in explanatory (not naming) context: **שער הים** ("Sea Gate") — useful for explaining the meaning of the name to a Hebrew-speaking group, but the site itself should still be referred to by its transliterated proper name, Porta Marina, since that is what appears on all maps, tickets and signage.
- **וִיָה** (via) — Latin/Italian for "street/road," as in Via Marina, the road leading from the gate.
- **צִינְטָה מוּרָרְיָה / חוֹמַת הָעִיר** (cinta muraria) — Italian term for "city wall circuit," relevant when describing the fortifications flanking the gate; in practice Israeli guides usually just say "חומות פומפיי" (Pompeii's walls) rather than transliterating the Italian term.
- **צִיפּוּס** (cippus) — Latin term for the inscribed boundary/marker stone (the Suedius Clemens cippus near the gate); commonly left untranslated even in Hebrew explanations, sometimes glossed as "אבן גבול" (boundary stone).
