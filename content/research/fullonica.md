## What you see

- **The converted impluvium**: the atrium's original rainwater-collection basin (impluvium) had its walls built up and was turned into a large rectangular masonry vat for fulling — one of the clearest "house-into-factory" details on site. In situ. [pompeiiinpictures.com p2](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p2.htm) · [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/fullonica-of-stephanus)
- **Three intercommunicating stone rinsing basins** in the rear garden/peristyle area, arranged so water (and dirt) moved from one to the next. In situ, though partly reconstructed/consolidated by excavators. [researchgate.net (Flohr plan)](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Plan-of-fullonica-of-Stephanus-I67-Pompeii-Source-Miko-Flohr_fig5_309666989) · [gpsmycity.com](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/fullonica-di-stephanus-(laundry-of-stephanus)-5958.html)
- **Five treading stalls/basins** next to the rinsing tubs, where workers stood and trod cloth by foot in a mix of water, fuller's earth and urine. In situ. [gpsmycity.com](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/fullonica-di-stephanus-(laundry-of-stephanus)-5958.html) · [researchgate.net (Flohr)](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Plan-of-fullonica-of-Stephanus-I67-Pompeii-Source-Miko-Flohr_fig5_309666989)
- **Elevated walkways and small staircases** around the basins, built so workers could reach in without falling into the vats. In situ. [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/fullonica-of-stephanus)
- **Reconstructed compluvium with original terracotta water-spouts**, through which rainwater once fell into the impluvium/vat — a mix of original spout fragments and modern reconstruction of the roof opening. [pompeiiinpictures.com p2](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p2.htm)
- **A pressing-room (with torcular/press remains)** to the left of the entrance, evidence of a mechanical press used to finish and flatten cloth. In situ (remains only — no complete standing press). [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/fullonica-of-stephanus)
- **Decorated oecus (reception room) walls**, Fourth-Style paintings on a black ground: birds, an ivy omphalos, plants, flying cupids, a Bacchic scene of a chariot pulled by panthers, and winged female figures interpreted as the Seasons. These are painted plaster still in situ on the walls, not narrative "fulling" scenes. [pompeiiinpictures.com p3](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p3.htm) · [gpsmycity.com](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/fullonica-di-stephanus-(laundry-of-stephanus)-5958.html)
- **Painted electoral notices (programmata)** near the entrance, including "Fullones universi rog(ant)" ("the fullers as a body ask [you to elect...]") and "Stephanus rog(at)", catalogued as CIL IV 7164–7173. In situ (painted on the facade plaster, condition varies). [pompeiiinpictures.com p1](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p1.htm) · [Ancient Graffiti Project listing](http://www.ancientgraffiti.org/Graffiti/graffito/search?city=Pompeii)
- **Important disambiguation for guiding:** the famous painted scene showing fullers actually *at work* — treading cloth in a tub, carding, inspecting fabric, working a press, with an owl (symbol of Minerva) atop a bleaching frame — is **not from this building**. It comes from a different Pompeii workshop, the Fullonica of M. Vesonius Primus / Veranius Hypsaeus (VI.8.20–21), and the original fresco is now in the Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli (MANN), inv. 9774. It is widely (and misleadingly) used as the generic illustration for "Pompeii fullers," including for Stephanus's workshop, so flag clearly on-site that it is not physically here. [researchgate.net (fresco fig.)](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Lower-section-of-the-fresco-of-the-so-called-Pilastro-dei-Fullones-from-the-fullonica-of_fig1_329726292) · [pompeiiinpictures VI.8.20](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/r6/6%2008%2020%20p2.htm)

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second intro.** This building started life as an ordinary Roman townhouse, with an atrium, small garden and living rooms. At some point before AD 79 — likely in the aftermath of the AD 62 earthquake — its owners gutted the domestic core and turned it into a working fullonica: a combined laundry and cloth-finishing workshop. It was still fully operational, urine-vats and all, the day Vesuvius erupted. [worldhistory.org](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11257/the-fullonica-of-stephanus-in-pompeii/)

**(b) 2-minute story (~150 words).** Fulling — treading raw or worn wool cloth in water, fuller's earth (creta fullonica) and stale urine to clean, degrease and felt it — was a real Pompeian industry, and this house shows it happening inside a converted home rather than a purpose-built factory. The atrium's impluvium, meant to collect rainwater for a family, was walled up into an industrial vat; the back garden became a bank of rinsing tubs and treading stalls; upper floors and a flat roof were used for drying and brushing finished cloth. Urine for the process was collected from public jars and urinals around town — a genuinely civic-economic detail, later echoed in Rome's imperial urine tax. The workshop, excavated in 1912–1914 along Via dell'Abbondanza, was buried mid-operation in AD 79, with painted election slogans on its facade naming "Stephanus" and "all the fullers" as a body. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p1.htm) · [seepompeii.com](https://seepompeii.com/en/luoghi/the-fullonica-of-stephanus-of-pompeii/)

**(c) Deep-dive (~250 words).** The fulling sequence, as reconstructed archaeologically: workers stood in individual treading stalls (five here) and trod cloth barefoot in a mix of water, fuller's earth and urine, whose ammonia cut grease and lanolin from the wool; the cloth then moved through a chain of connecting rinsing basins (three here), clean water entering at one end so cloth progressed from dirtiest to cleanest water; it was then stretched, brushed with teasels or thistles to raise the nap, sometimes sulfur-bleached, and finally pressed — hence the pressing-room evidence near the entrance. Urine was not made on-site in bulk; ancient sources and modern scholarship agree fullers relied on urine collected from public latrines and street jars, a trade later shadowed by Rome's *vectigal urinae*, a tax on public-urinal proceeds first attributed to Nero and re-imposed by Vespasian (c. AD 70) — the origin of the "pecunia non olet" ("money doesn't stink") anecdote in Suetonius. Whether that specific imperial tax applied to fullers' urine supply in Pompeii itself is not directly attested and should be treated as context, not proven local fact. Stephanus is known only from painted electoral programmata on the facade ("Stephanus rog(at)"); scholars (Della Corte; more cautiously Varone and Stefani) debate whether he was owner or manager. Miko Flohr's fieldwork (the "Cleaning the Laundries" project, 2006–2008) and his book *The World of the Fullo* (Oxford, 2013) reassess fullones as a more socially and economically integrated trade than older "smelly, low-status" narratives assumed, and specifically argue Pompeii's fullonicae combined fabric care/finishing services ("consumption") with textile production — a debate still open. [oup.com](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-world-of-the-fullo-9780199659357) · [Wikipedia: Pecunia non olet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecunia_non_olet) · [Wikipedia: Fullo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullo)

## The detail most visitors miss

Look left of the entrance doorway on the street facade: faint painted red-letter election notices survive, including "FULLONES UNIVERSI ROG(ANT)" — "the fullers, all of them together, ask [you to vote for...]". It is one of the only places at Pompeii where an entire trade guild, not just an individual, publicly endorses a political candidate on a wall — direct evidence that this workshop's workers organized and acted as a civic body, not just laborers. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p1.htm)

## Anecdotes

- Near the entrance, excavators found a skeleton together with roughly 1,089–1,090 sesterces in coins — very possibly Stephanus (or whoever was minding the till) caught trying to flee with the day's takings, or simply sheltering by the strongbox when the pyroclastic surge hit. The identification with Stephanus himself is a plausible but unproven inference, not a certainty. [gpsmycity.com](https://www.gpsmycity.com/attractions/fullonica-di-stephanus-(laundry-of-stephanus)-5958.html) · [seepompeii.com](https://seepompeii.com/en/luoghi/the-fullonica-of-stephanus-of-pompeii/)
- The everyday "ick factor" is real and well documented: Roman fullers ran on human urine collected from public jars/urinals placed around Roman towns, prized specifically for its ammonia content as a grease-cutting, mildly bleaching agent — a genuinely popular and camera-friendly fact, not an exaggeration. [historyoftheancientworld.com](https://www.historyoftheancientworld.com/2011/02/cleverness-cleanliness-and-urine-in-ancient-rome/) · [Wikipedia: Fullo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullo)
- **Disputed legend, flagged as such:** the "fullers at work" fresco so often shown in guidebooks for Stephanus's laundry (a fuller treading in a tub, another inspecting cloth under an owl-topped frame) is genuinely from Pompeii, but from a *different* fullonica — that of Veranius Hypsaeus (VI.8.20–21) — and now hangs in MANN Naples, not here. Guides and even some tourist sites conflate the two buildings; treat any claim that this specific fresco is "in this room" as incorrect. [researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Lower-section-of-the-fresco-of-the-so-called-Pilastro-dei-Fullones-from-the-fullonica-of_fig1_329726292)

## FAQ

- **Did they really clean clothes with urine here?** Yes — stale urine (a natural ammonia source) mixed with water and fuller's earth was standard for degreasing and whitening wool cloth in Roman fulling, collected from public urinals/jars around the city. [historyoftheancientworld.com](https://www.historyoftheancientworld.com/2011/02/cleverness-cleanliness-and-urine-in-ancient-rome/)
- **Was this building always a laundry?** No — it began as an ordinary atrium-plan private house and was converted into an industrial fullonica, likely in the years after the AD 62 earthquake, with the atrium's impluvium walled up into a fulling vat. [worldhistory.org](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11257/the-fullonica-of-stephanus-in-pompeii/) · [italyguides.it](https://www.italyguides.it/en/campania/pompeii/fullonica-of-stephanus)
- **Who was Stephanus?** He is known only from painted electoral endorsements on the facade naming "Stephanus"; scholars debate whether he was the workshop's owner or its manager, and a skeleton with coins found near the entrance is tentatively, not certainly, linked to him. [pompeiiinpictures.com](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R1/1%2006%2007%20p1.htm)
- **Is the famous painting of fullers at work here?** No — that fresco belongs to a different building, the Fullonica of Veranius Hypsaeus (VI.8.20–21), and is displayed in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN), not on-site at I.6.7. [researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Lower-section-of-the-fresco-of-the-so-called-Pilastro-dei-Fullones-from-the-fullonica-of_fig1_329726292)

## Hebrew naming

פוּלוֹנִיקָה (fullonica) — בית המלאכה לכיבוס וגימור בדי צמר ברומא העתיקה. ברבים: פוּלוֹנִיקַאוּי (fullonicae).
פוּלוֹ (fullo), ברבים פוּלוֹנֶס (fullones) — "הכובס"/"מכבס הצמר", בעל המלאכה או הפועל בה.
סְטֶפַנוּס (Stephanus) — שם בעל בית המלאכה, כפי שמופיע בכתובות בחירות שנמצאו על חזית הבניין.
אֶטְרִיוּם (atrium), אִימְפְּלוּבְיוּם (impluvium) — חלל הכניסה המרכזי בבית רומי והבריכה לאיסוף מי גשמים במרכזו, ששימשה כאן כאגן תעשייתי.
בהדרכות עבריות נפוץ גם התיאור התיאורי "המכבסה של סטפנוס" או "בית המלאכה לכיבוס צמר" במקום תעתיק מדויק של fullonica.
