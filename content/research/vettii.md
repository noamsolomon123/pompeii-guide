# House of the Vettii — Regio VI

## Identity

**English name:** House of the Vettii.
**Italian name:** Casa dei Vettii.
**Regio/insula/number:** Regio VI, Insula 15, entrance no. 1 (catalogue code VI.15.1) — the house occupies the entire southern section of insula 15, bounded by Vicolo di Mercurio and Vicolo dei Vettii. Source: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii); [Wikidata Q1590855](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1590855) (street address "Regio VI – Insula 15 – 1"); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).

**GPS:** 40°45′07.5″N, 14°29′04.5″E → 40.752083, 14.484583 decimal degrees. Source: [Wikidata Q1590855](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1590855) coordinate-location statement (cross-checked against [latitude.to/House of the Vettii](https://latitude.to/articles-by-country/it/italy/14287/house-of-the-vettii), which independently gives 40.7521, 14.4846). This is the site/building centroid, not "approx" — the two independent sources agree to within a few meters.

**Area:** approx. 1,100–1,124 m² (sources differ slightly: Wikidata gives 1,124 m²; Italian Wikipedia gives "circa 1.100 m²"). One of the largest private houses in Pompeii. Source: [Wikidata Q1590855](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1590855); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).

The visit date used here, **20 October 2026, falls in the winter schedule**. Source: [pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/). ## What you see

- **The Priapus fresco at the entrance vestibule** (north-west corner, just inside the doorway from Vicolo dei Vettii): the fertility god Priapus, bearded, weighing his oversized phallus on one pan of a set of scales against a bag of money/coins on the other pan; a basket of fruit beside him symbolizes the house's abundance, and attributes of Mercury (a ram, a vase, a caduceus, a purse) reinforce the owners' mercantile identity. It functioned as an apotropaic (evil-eye-warding) doorstep image and a blunt announcement of the owners' wealth. It is openly displayed today, unlike for much of the 20th century (see Anecdotes). Sources: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VI.15.1 entrance](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2015%2001%20entrance.htm); [worldhistory.org, Priapus Fresco](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/11277/priapus-fresco/); [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii).
- **Vivid Fourth-Style "Pompeian red" and black-ground wall paintings** covering nearly every room facing the peristyle, freshly re-emerged after the 2020–2023 restoration removed decades-old protective wax coatings that had dulled the colors. Source: [theartnewspaper.com](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/01/11/vast-pompeii-residence-unveiled-with-panel-depicting-a-giant-penis) (restoration director Arianna Spinosa quoted on colors "re-emerging" from under 20th-century wax layers).
- **Twelve large mythological panel paintings** in Fourth Style, spread across the two triclinia and adjoining rooms — among them the Punishment of Ixion (bound to a fiery wheel), Daedalus presenting Pasiphae with the wooden cow, Dionysus discovering the sleeping Ariadne, the Death of Pentheus (torn apart by maenads), the Punishment of Dirce (tied to a bull), the infant Hercules strangling the snakes sent to kill him, Pan wrestling Eros, Cyparissus mourning his stag, Achilles unmasked on Skyros, and Hero and Leander. Sources: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii) (Italian Wikipedia gives room-by-room attributions: triclinium P = Ixion/Pasiphae/Ariadne cycle; oecus N = Dirce/Pentheus/infant Hercules cycle).
- **The "Salone degli Amorini" (Hall of the Cupids/Salone Q)** — a frieze running around the room showing cupids and psyches engaged in the everyday trades of Pompeii's economy: selling wine, cleaning textiles, harvesting grapes, goldsmithing, growing and selling flowers, and making perfume — a self-portrait, in miniature, of the commercial world that made the Vettii rich. Source: [pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).
- **The peristyle garden**, the true centerpiece of the house (the owners removed the traditional tablinum to enlarge it): restored and replanted after 2020 with ivy, butcher's-broom, acanthus, papyrus, daisies, myrtle, roses, juniper and santolina, using plants raised in the park's own nursery; the garden retains its marble tables, two herm pillars (Bacchus/Ariadne and Silenus/a maenad) and basins. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66783).
- **What is NOT original on site:** the garden's original marble fountains, basins, pilasters and free-standing statues (12 pieces recorded from the 19th-century excavation — 7 marble, 2 bronze, 2 fragmentary, 1 lost) are held at the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN); faithful copies now stand in their place in the restored garden. This is a partial exception to the house's overall reputation — most of its painted decoration, unlike the garden statuary, is original and has never left the walls it was painted on. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66783).
- **The kitchen (room W)**, where excavators found two iron tripods, an iron grill, five bronze cooking pots and ceramic vessels in place, plus a marble statue of an ithyphallic Priapus and a satyr; the floor is a plain cocciopesto (crushed-terracotta) surface inlaid with small colored stone chips. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).
- **A small erotic cubiculum (room X′)**, identified — via a graffito naming a woman, Eutychis, of apparently Greek origin — as the likely workspace of an enslaved prostitute; its white-ground walls carry three modestly-executed erotic scenes, and a painted owl beside a niche served an apotropaic, evil-eye-warding function, matching the Priapus fresco's protective logic at the front door. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii); [pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/).

## History in layers

**(a) 30-second version:**
This 1,100 m² mansion was built not by hereditary nobility but by two formerly enslaved men who became rich as merchants — and it shows: dense, wall-to-wall mythological frescoes and a lavish peristyle garden, all remodeled after the earthquake of AD 62 and then buried by Vesuvius in AD 79. Excavated in 1894–1896, it is famous for having almost all of its original decoration still in place. After roughly two decades largely closed for structural and conservation work, it reopened to the public on 10 January 2023.

**(b) 2-minute version (~150 words):**
The house takes its name from its owners, Aulus Vettius Restitutus and Aulus Vettius Conviva, both freedmen — former slaves who had been legally freed and gone on to build fortunes, likely in the wine trade, based on bronze seals and wax tablets bearing their names found on site. Pompeii's official park description calls them brothers, though some scholarship treats that as unproven — they may simply be two men freed by, and sharing the family name of, the same former owner. Whatever their exact relationship, their house is treated as a case study in nouveau-riche Pompeian society: no ancestral pedigree, but enormous spending on Greek mythological wall paintings and a showpiece garden to signal new status. Excavated by Italian authorities between 1894 and 1896 (accounts differ slightly on the precise start/end months), the house preserved its decoration almost intact because, unlike many 18th-century Pompeian digs, its contents were left in place rather than stripped for museum collections. Sources: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii); [pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).

**(c) ~250-word deep-dive:**
The Vettii brothers' wealth without inherited status was typical of a real social phenomenon in Roman Campania: freedmen (liberti) were legally barred from holding most public magistracies, but could accumulate serious money in trade, and often funneled it into private luxury and into quasi-civic religious roles — Restitutus is attested with the title of Augustalis, a priesthood of the imperial cult specifically open to freedmen otherwise excluded from office. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii). Their house is decorated in the Fourth Pompeian Style, the last and most theatrical of the four canonical wall-painting styles, which layers faux marble paneling, illusionistic architecture and large framed mythological "picture panels" believed to copy lost Greek originals; art historians read the house's specific choice of scenes — Ixion, Pentheus, Dirce, the infant Hercules — as a thematic program of divine punishment and reward. Source: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii). The entrance Priapus fresco has its own separate, well-documented display history: a 1968 photograph shows it kept inside a lockable wooden case with a shutter, opened for visitors who tipped the custodian, and a 19th-century iron door restricted access to the adjacent erotic-themed room to male visitors "on request" — both restrictions were lifted as part of the most recent restoration. Sources: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VI.15.1 entrance](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2015%2001%20entrance.htm); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66783). That final restoration ran roughly August 2020–January 2023 (after a partial 2016 reopening and a further closure), replacing a failing 1950s reinforced-concrete roof with wood/tile, installing LED lighting and integrated photovoltaic roof tiles, removing old wax coatings from the frescoes, and replanting the garden using the park's own nursery stock; it reopened 10 January 2023 with park director Gabriel Zuchtriegel calling it "the house which tells the story of Roman society." No official restoration cost figure or visitor-count statistic was found in the sources checked for this research — flagged as **not available/unverified**. Sources: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii); [theartnewspaper.com](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/01/11/vast-pompeii-residence-unveiled-with-panel-depicting-a-giant-penis); [smithsonianmag.com](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/after-decades-of-restoration-lavish-pompeii-house-reopens-to-public-180981419/).

## The detail most visitors miss

Inside the small erotic cubiculum (room X′, off the smaller of the two peristyles), look for a painted owl beside a wall niche — easy to miss next to the room's more attention-grabbing erotic scenes. It served the same protective, evil-eye-warding purpose as the famous Priapus fresco at the front door, but almost nobody stops to notice this second, quieter apotropaic image tucked in a back room. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).

## Anecdotes

1. **The Priapus fresco was, for decades, shown only for a tip.** A 1968 photograph documented on pompeiiinpictures.com shows the entrance fresco enclosed behind a lockable wooden shutter/case — tourists had to persuade (and often pay) the site custodian to unlock it before they could see it. That physical restriction is gone today; the fresco has been openly visible in photographs from 2015 onward. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VI.15.1 entrance](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2015%2001%20entrance.htm).
2. **An iron door once made a whole room "men only."** In the 19th century, an iron door was fitted to restrict entry to the erotically-frescoed room, admitting only male visitors, and only on request — mirroring the far more famous "Gabinetto Segreto" (Secret Cabinet) of erotic Pompeian art locked away at the Naples Archaeological Museum. The door was removed as part of the modern restoration/reopening. Source: [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66783); background on the Naples "Secret Cabinet" comparison: [atlasobscura.com/places/gabinetto-segreto](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/gabinetto-segreto).
3. **Were the Vettii really brothers?** — **disputed.** Pompeii's official park page (pompeiisites.org) describes Restitutus and Conviva simply as "brothers." Wikipedia's English article, citing a 2023 Smithsonian piece, instead frames them as "likely unrelated individuals... enslaved by the same person" who both took the family name Vettius from a shared former owner upon manumission — a very common pattern for Roman freedmen, and not evidence of blood relation. Treat "brothers" as the popular/official framing, not a settled scholarly fact. Sources: [pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/](https://pompeiisites.org/sito_archeologico/casa-dei-vettii/); [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii); [smithsonianmag.com](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/after-decades-of-restoration-lavish-pompeii-house-reopens-to-public-180981419/).

## FAQ

**Q: Why was the House of the Vettii closed for so long?**
A: It was shut to the public in stages from 2002 onward over structural safety concerns (a deteriorating 1950s reinforced-concrete roof and water infiltration), briefly and partially reopened in December 2016, then closed again for a definitive, multi-year restoration that ran into 2023. Source: [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66783).

**Q: Is the Priapus fresco visible to visitors now?**
A: Yes — since the January 2023 reopening it is openly displayed at the entrance with no shutter, case, or access restriction, unlike the locked-case/tip-to-view arrangement documented as late as 1968. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com, VI.15.1 entrance](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R6/6%2015%2001%20entrance.htm).

**Q: Do I need a separate ticket for this house?**
A: No — it's inside the main walled archaeological site and included in the standard Pompeii entrance ticket (€20 full price as of the January 2026 price list; no supplement for this house specifically). Source: [pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/).

**Q: Is most of what I'm seeing original, or reconstructed?**
A: The painted decoration — the frescoes on the walls — is overwhelmingly original and has remained in place since excavation in 1894–1896, which is exactly why the house is so celebrated. The main documented exception is the garden's marble/bronze statuary and fountains, whose originals are now in the Naples Archaeological Museum (MANN) and have been replaced on site with copies. Source: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Vettii); [it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dei_Vettii).

## Hebrew naming

- **House of the Vettii / Casa dei Vettii** → בית הוֶטִי (Beit ha-Veti) or transliterated directly as קאזה דיי וטי (Casa dei Vettii) — Israeli tour guides commonly use the transliterated Italian form "קאזה דיי וטי" interchangeably with the descriptive Hebrew "בית הוטי."
- **Atrium** → אטריום (Atrium) — standard transliteration, in wide guiding use.
- **Peristyle** (colonnaded garden court) → פריסטיל (Peristil).
- **Triclinium** (formal dining room) → טריקליניום (Triklinium).
- **Priapus** → פריאפוס (Priapus) — transliterated; the fresco itself is often referred to in guiding Hebrew simply as "הפרסקו של פריאפוס."
- **Freedman / liberti** → משוחרר (pl. משוחררים) for "freedman/freedmen" in plain Hebrew, or the transliterated Latin term ליברטוס / ליברטי (libertus/liberti) in more academic guiding contexts.
- **Fresco** → פרסקו (Fresco).
- **Lararium** (household shrine) → לררium is not standard; Israeli guides typically say "מקדש הבית" (mikdash ha-bayit, "house shrine") or transliterate as לרריום (Lararium).
- **Fourth (Pompeian) Style** → הסגנון הרביעי (הפומפיאני) — "the fourth [Pompeian] style," used descriptively rather than as a fixed guiding term.
