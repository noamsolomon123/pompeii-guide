# Central Baths (Terme Centrali)

## Identity

- **Official name (EN):** Central Baths. **Italian:** Terme Centrali.
- **Regio/Insula:** Regio IX, Insula 4 — the baths occupy the *entire* insula, at the corner of Via di Nola (north side) and Via Stabiana (west side). Main street entrance "a" opens onto Via di Nola at doorway **IX.4.18**; a second entrance into the palaestra opens from Via Stabiana at **IX.4.5**; a further entrance into the palaestra, past a multi-seat latrine, is on the unnamed vicolo on the south side at **IX.4.10**; service doorways for the furnace/garden area sit on the east side at IX.4.15–16. ## What you see

1. **Unfinished brick walls with no final decorative facing** — the walls throughout are bare opus latericium (brickwork), never covered with the marble revetment or fine plaster that finished baths elsewhere in Pompeii would have had. In-situ. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
2. **The caldarium (hot room, "s") without its marble cladding** — the room is structurally complete (hypocaust floor, flue tiles) but was never given its final marble finish before the eruption. In-situ, visible today as bare brick/masonry. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
3. **Unusually large window openings** — three large windows in the tepidarium opening onto the palaestra, three large windows plus five smaller ones in the caldarium (angled to catch the afternoon/evening sun), and three round windows in the laconicum — a sharp contrast with the small, high windows of Pompeii's older baths (Forum Baths, Stabian Baths). In-situ as openings in the brick walls (any glazing itself is not preserved). Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
4. **A shallow depression in the ground on the east side of the palaestra** marking where the planned natatio (open-air swimming pool) was to be built — it was never completed. In-situ. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.5](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2005.htm).
5. **Remains of older, demolished houses still visible within the palaestra** — evidence that the whole insula was cleared for this single project and the clearing/rebuilding was still in progress. In-situ. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.5](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2005.htm).
6. **The laconicum ("r"), a small circular sweat room** with four semicircular niches and a partly-preserved vaulted ceiling — one of the more architecturally complete rooms. In-situ. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
7. **The frigidarium/apodyterium basin ("p")**, a large rectangular cold-water basin roughly 1.5 m (nearly 5 feet) deep, with three windows lighting it. In-situ. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
8. **A cast of a child's skeleton**, found in 2018 near an entrance of the baths and now displayed/discussed on site as physical evidence of a victim who sheltered in the (unfinished, roofless-in-parts) building during the eruption. In-situ find-spot; check current on-site display status when visiting. Source: [The Local, 26 Apr 2018](https://www.thelocal.it/20180426/child-skeleton-discovered-pompeii); [pompeiisites.org press kit](https://pompeiisites.org/press-kit/http-pompeiisites-org-comunicati-la-piccola-vittima-delle-terme-centrali/).

## History in layers

### 30-second version
The Central Baths were Pompeii's newest, biggest, and most modern bathhouse — started after the earthquake of AD 62 and never finished. When Vesuvius erupted in AD 79, the building was still a construction site: bare brick walls, no marble, an unbuilt swimming pool. It was never used for its intended purpose even once.

### 2-minute version
After the earthquake of AD 62 badly damaged Pompeii, the city entered a major rebuilding phase. Sometime in that period, an entire city block (Insula 4, Regio IX) at a central crossroads — Via di Nola and Via Stabiana — was cleared of its older houses to make way for a single, ambitious new bath complex. Unlike Pompeii's older baths, which had separate wings for men and women, the Central Baths were designed as one large circuit meant to serve both sexes through time-shifted access, and they were built with far larger windows for natural light, drawing on newer, more "metropolitan" Roman bathing architecture. But the project was still incomplete 17 years later: the walls lacked their planned marble facing, a large open-air swimming pool (natatio) on the east side of the palaestra was never built, and two furnaces needed to heat the water had not been installed. When Vesuvius erupted on 24 August AD 79 (per the traditional date; some scholarship debates the exact month), the Central Baths had never opened to the public. The site was excavated in three main campaigns: 1817, 1836, and 1877–1878. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm); [ermakvagus.com](https://ermakvagus.com/Europe/Italy/Pompeii/central.html).

### Deep-dive (~250 words)
The Central Baths are the single clearest surviving example of Pompeii's post-AD 62 rebuilding program. After the earthquake, large parts of the city were under repair or redevelopment when the AD 79 eruption struck, and most sites show this only as patched masonry or partial repairs; the Central Baths, by contrast, are a wholesale new-build project caught mid-construction, making them an unusually direct archaeological window into how, and how fast, Pompeii was rebuilding itself in its final years. Architecturally, the complex breaks from Pompeii's earlier bath tradition (Forum Baths, Stabian Baths), which used small, high-set windows and duplicated suites of rooms to physically separate men's and women's bathing. The Central Baths instead were planned around one unified, more spacious circuit shared by both sexes at different times of day, and were lit by unusually large windows — three large openings in the tepidarium, three large plus five smaller in the caldarium (oriented to capture the afternoon sun), and three round windows in the laconicum. Scholars connect this "open," light-filled, single-circuit design to newer bathing models circulating in the wider Roman world (sometimes compared to Rome's imperial thermae), suggesting Pompeii's elite or civic patrons were reaching for a genuinely up-to-date building type rather than repeating older local designs. How close to completion the building was, and who was funding or overseeing such a large public project so late in the city's life, remain matters that are estimated/disputed rather than settled in the sources checked here. In 2018, restoration and consolidation work at the site led to the discovery — 20 years after the last complete skeleton find at Pompeii — of a child's remains near an entrance, prompting fresh interdisciplinary study of the building and its final hours. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm); [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.5](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2005.htm); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/51275); [The Local](https://www.thelocal.it/20180426/child-skeleton-discovered-pompeii).

## The detail most visitors miss

Look at the ground on the **east side of the open courtyard (palaestra)**: there is a shallow, roughly rectangular depression in the earth. This is not erosion — it is the footprint marking exactly where the natatio (open-air swimming pool) was laid out and about to be built when work stopped. It is one of the few places in Pompeii where you can stand inside a Roman building's construction blueprint rather than its finished form. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.5](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2005.htm).

## Anecdotes

1. In April 2018, during consolidation and restoration work at the Central Baths, archaeologists using a videoscope to inspect an inaccessible area found the skull, then the rest of the skeleton, of a child aged about 7–8, buried just 10 cm below an entrance surface — an unusual, shallow find compared with the thick pumice/ash sequences typical elsewhere at Pompeii. It was the first complete skeleton found at the site in roughly 20 years, and the first child's remains in about 50. Then-director Massimo Osanna called it an "exceptional discovery." Source: [The Local, 26 Apr 2018](https://www.thelocal.it/20180426/child-skeleton-discovered-pompeii); [thehistoryblog.com](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/51275); [pompeiisites.org press kit](https://pompeiisites.org/press-kit/http-pompeiisites-org-comunicati-la-piccola-vittima-delle-terme-centrali/).
2. The Central Baths were designed with no separate men's and women's wings at all — a genuine architectural novelty for Pompeii — with bathers instead meant to use the single circuit at different scheduled times of day. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
3. Even after nearly two millennia buried and excavated, the Central Baths suffered a second blow in the 20th century: the Pompeii archaeological area, including structures in this part of the site, sustained damage from Allied bombing raids during World War II in 1943 (part of the campaign supporting the invasion of Italy), which is documented for the site generally though the exact building-by-building damage list for the Central Baths specifically was not confirmed in sources checked — flagged as a general-site fact, not baths-specific confirmed. Source: [Open Culture, WWII bombing of Pompeii](https://www.openculture.com/2021/10/the-bombing-of-pompeii-during-world-war-ii.html); [pompeiiperspectives.org](https://www.pompeiiperspectives.org/index.php/wwii-bomb-damage).

## FAQ

1. **Why does this bathhouse look unfinished, while others in Pompeii look "complete"?** Because it genuinely was unfinished — construction began only after the AD 62 earthquake and was still underway when Vesuvius erupted in AD 79, so it never received its planned marble wall-facings, its swimming pool, or its furnaces. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
2. **Did anyone ever actually bathe here?** Not found in sources checked as a confirmed "yes" — the building appears to have never opened to the public before the eruption, based on its incomplete state (no marble facing, no pool, no furnaces installed). Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
3. **How is this different from the Forum Baths or Stabian Baths?** Those older baths have small, high windows and fully separate suites for men and women; the Central Baths were planned with much larger windows for natural light and a single shared circuit used by both sexes on a time-shift basis rather than duplicate wings. Source: [pompeiiinpictures.com IX.4.18](https://pompeiiinpictures.com/pompeiiinpictures/R9/9%2004%2018.htm).
4. **Is the child's skeleton found here on display?** A cast/find from the 2018 discovery near the baths' entrance has been documented by the Archaeological Park; current on-site display status should be checked at pompeiisites.org or with park staff at time of visit, as this was not confirmed for 2026 in sources checked. Source: [pompeiisites.org press kit](https://pompeiisites.org/press-kit/http-pompeiisites-org-comunicati-la-piccola-vittima-delle-terme-centrali/); [The Local](https://www.thelocal.it/20180426/child-skeleton-discovered-pompeii).

## Hebrew naming

טרמה צנטרלי / "המרחצאות המרכזיים" — התרגום המקובל ל-Central Baths / Terme Centrali. בהדרכות בעברית נהוג להשתמש בשם האנגלי-לטיני "Central Baths" לצד התרגום העברי.

מונחי יסוד בלטינית למתחמי מרחצאות רומיים (חוזרים לאורך כל הסיור):

- **קלדריום (Caldarium)** — חדר המים החמים.
- **טפידריום (Tepidarium)** — חדר הפושרים (הביניים).
- **פריגידריום (Frigidarium)** — חדר המים הקרים.
- **אפודיטריום (Apodyterium)** — חדר ההלבשה/הפשטה.
- **נטציו (Natatio)** — בריכת שחייה פתוחה.
- **פלסטרה (Palaestra)** — חצר פתוחה/מגרש התעמלות.
- **לקוניקום (Laconicum)** — חדר הזעה עגול (מונח נוסף שמופיע במתחם הספציפי הזה).

הערה: מדריכים ישראלים רבים משאירים את המונחים הלטיניים כפי שהם (בתעתיק), ולא מתרגמים אותם לעברית, כדי לשמור על עקביות מול שלטי ההסבר באתר עצמו (שהם באיטלקית/אנגלית).
